# Criterion 12 — outreach messages

Pick one, send it, then send the explanation as a **separate** message so the ask
and the artefact don't blur together. Do not attach the explanation to the ask.

---

## A. Someone you know (student, clubmate, junior colleague)

> Hey — can I borrow ten minutes of your brain? I've got a page of text about an
> Arduino sensor circuit and I need to know whether it's actually clear to
> someone who didn't write it. No prep needed, and there's no right answer I'm
> fishing for. I'll send it over and ask you two questions after.

---

## B. Faculty

> Sir/Ma'am — I'm working on a hardware design tool and I need one thing I can't
> produce myself: an outside read.
>
> I have a one-page explanation of an Arduino + DHT22 temperature circuit. I'd
> like ten minutes of your time to read it and answer two questions about whether
> the reasoning holds up. I'm deliberately not giving you any context beforehand,
> because context is exactly what I'm testing for the absence of.
>
> Ten minutes, no preparation, and I'm happy to come to you.

---

## C. Electronics / robotics club

> Looking for 2–3 people who can read a schematic, for ten minutes each.
>
> I'll hand you a page explaining an Arduino + DHT22 circuit and ask two
> questions about it afterwards. I can't tell you anything beforehand — the point
> is to find out whether the page stands on its own. Nothing to prepare, and it's
> genuinely ten minutes.

---

## D. r/AskElectronics or EEVblog (asynchronous fallback)

Post title:

> Does this explanation of an Arduino + DHT22 circuit actually hold up?

Body:

> Below is a written explanation of a simple Arduino + DHT22 temperature-alert
> circuit. I'd like a sanity check from people who work with these parts:
>
> 1. Is the reasoning for each component choice correct?
> 2. Is anything stated as a consequence actually wrong — i.e. would the failure
>    it describes really happen?
>
> I've left out where it came from on purpose so it gets read on its own merits.
> Happy to say afterwards.
>
> [paste explanation verbatim]

On these forums people will also volunteer *technical* corrections, which is a
bonus the in-person version won't give you — an actual error in the explanation
is more valuable than a pass.

---

## The follow-up, after they answer

> That was written by the tool I'm building — it generates the circuit, the
> firmware and that explanation from a one-line prompt. I didn't say so upfront
> because I wanted an unbiased read. Thank you, genuinely; that's the one test I
> couldn't run myself.

Ask it in this order every time: **answers first, disclosure second, discussion
third.** Disclosing early costs you the result.
