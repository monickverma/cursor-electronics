# Layout → circuit back-annotation for small 2-layer PCBs: parasitics, PI, SI residue (free tools, 2026)

> Provenance key used throughout. **[F]** = I fetched the primary page this session (only the gerber2ems README and the padne README). **[S]** = taken from a web-search result summary of the linked page; the page itself was not opened, so wording and numbers should be re-checked before being hard-coded. **[calc]** = my own arithmetic from a cited or recalled formula. **[recall]** = from training knowledge, NOT source-verified this session (these live only under Inferences/Gaps). Research budget was ~19 tool calls; several key questions were only partly covered and the gaps are listed explicitly.

## 1. Closed-form formulas: accuracy, validity ranges, and free Python implementations

### Takeaway
Only a handful of closed forms were source-confirmed this session (Hammerstad-Jensen microstrip accuracy, Johnson & Graham via inductance, IPC-2221 current formula); they are sufficient to show that on this board class every per-trace parasitic is 2+ orders below component tolerance, but most of the requested formula catalogue (Ruehli/Grover bar inductance, coplanar loop L, crosstalk, pad capacitance) and the Python-library survey remain unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Hammerstad-Jensen microstrip equations: stated error "better than 0.01% for W/h ≤ 1 and 0.03% for W/h ≤ 1000" (this is the error of the closed form against the underlying quasi-static reference solution, for zero-thickness strip; it is not an accuracy-against-measurement claim). The model is built from a homogeneous-medium impedance equation plus an effective-permittivity equation. [S] — [Qucs technical papers, microstrip](https://qucs.sourceforge.net/tech/node75.html)
- IPC-2141 microstrip formula is less accurate than Wheeler's equations; Wheeler is quoted as "a factor ~10 higher accuracy (less than 0.7% error)" versus numerical results. [S] — [Altium, Clearing Up Trace Impedance Calculators and Formulas](https://resources.altium.com/p/clearing-up-trace-impedance-calculators-and-formulas)
- Different online microstrip calculators give materially different answers for identical inputs (forum evidence, low weight). [S] — [EEVblog thread](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/projects/microstrip-calculators-inaccurate)
- Via self-inductance (Johnson & Graham, *High-Speed Digital Design*, 1993): L[nH] = 5.08 · h · [ln(4h/d) + 1], h = via length and d = hole diameter in inches; 5.08 is µ0/2π in nH/inch. Worked check: h = 0.04 in, d = 0.01 in → 0.77 nH. Validity: h ≫ d, thin wall; length dominates (diameter is inside a log). [S] — [Signal Consulting (H. Johnson), Via Inductance](https://www.sigcon.com/Pubs/news/6_04.htm); [Via Inductance II](https://www.sigcon.com/Pubs/news/6_08.htm); [Cadence, via inductance rule of thumb](https://resources.system-analysis.cadence.com/blog/msa2021-is-there-a-via-inductance-rule-of-thumb)
- Same formula in cm with factor 2: a 0.157 cm (1.6 mm) board with a 0.041 cm via gives about 1.2 nH. [S] — [Altair PollEx, Via Inductance](https://2023.help.altair.com/2023/Pollex/topics/pollex/verification/dfe_item_via_inductance_r.htm)
- IPC-2221 current formula: I = k · ΔT^0.44 · A^0.725 (A in mil², ΔT in °C), k = 0.048 external, 0.024 internal. [S] — [Altium IPC-2221 calculator](https://resources.altium.com/p/ipc-2221-calculator-pcb-trace-current-and-heating)

### Inferences
- [calc, formula recalled] DC sheet resistance of 1 oz copper: ρ/t = 1.72e-8 Ω·m / 35 µm ≈ 0.49 mΩ/square at 20 °C. A 0.25 mm × 15 mm trace is 60 squares ≈ 29.5 mΩ, reproducing the earlier report's "29 mΩ vs 120 Ω" case (ratio 2.5e-4, i.e. 40× below a 1% resistor tolerance).
- [calc] Skin depth δ = sqrt(ρ/(π f µ0)) ≈ 66 µm at 1 MHz and ≈ 21 µm at 10 MHz. 35 µm copper therefore only starts to see AC resistance rise above roughly 3.5 MHz, and by at most a small factor at 10 MHz; with DC R already 2–3 orders below tolerance, AC resistance is not worth modelling.
- [calc from the cited J&G formula] A 1.6 mm through via with a 0.4 mm hole is ≈ 1.2 nH, i.e. 0.075 Ω at 10 MHz and 0.75 Ω at 100 MHz. Negligible in every signal path of the six templates; relevant only inside a decoupling loop.
- [calc, formula recalled] Via barrel resistance R = ρh / (π·(d+t)·t) with 20 µm plating, d = 0.4 mm, h = 1.6 mm ≈ 1.0 mΩ. Plating thickness is a fab parameter (commonly quoted 18–25 µm) and should be carried as an interval.
- [calc, simple Wheeler/Hammerstad form recalled] A 0.25 mm trace over a B.Cu pour through 1.6 mm FR-4 (εr ≈ 4.5): w/h = 0.156, εeff ≈ 2.95, Z0 ≈ 137 Ω, giving ≈ 0.042 pF/mm and ≈ 0.79 nH/mm. A 50 mm trace is therefore ≈ 2.1 pF and ≈ 39 nH. Against a 100 nF filter capacitor that is 2e-5 relative; against a 4.7 kΩ pull-up it adds a 10 ns time constant.
- Because hobby 2-layer traces sit at w/h ≈ 0.1–0.5 with Z0 ≈ 100–150 Ω, they are far from the 50 Ω region most calculators are tuned for; any library chosen must be valid at small w/h. Hammerstad-Jensen is (cited range up to W/h ≤ 1000 with no lower bound stated in the summary), whereas the IPC-2141 form is reported by a secondary source to be limited to roughly 0.1 < w/h < 2.
- For the proof layer a formula's own fit error is irrelevant next to input uncertainty: FR-4 εr (≈ 4.2–4.8), dielectric thickness (±10%), etched width and copper thickness dominate, so a ±20–30% interval on any C or L from a closed form is the defensible statement, not the 0.03% of the fit.

### Gaps
- **IPC-2141A 2018 errata removing accuracy claims**: not found; the search returned nothing on an errata. Unconfirmed — do not cite it as fact.
- Not verified this session (no source retrieved): Ruehli/Grover partial self-inductance of a rectangular bar and its validity range; loop inductance of two coplanar traces; trace-over-plane loop inductance formula; Johnson & Graham crosstalk estimate 1/(1+(D/H)²) and Bogatin's rules of thumb; pad-to-plane capacitance (parallel-plate + fringe); copper temperature coefficient (commonly quoted ≈ 0.39 %/°C).
- Python library survey not performed: scikit-rf `skrf.media` (MLine etc.), pcb-tools, atlc/atlc2, wcalc, pyems, "pcbtc", and whether KiCad's PCB Calculator TransLine engine can be called headless. [recall] KiCad's calculator is a GUI tool whose transline code derives from Qucs; I found no evidence either way of a headless/Python entry point this session.
- The secondary-source validity range for IPC-2141 (0.1–2.0 w/h, εr 1–15) carried no citation to IPC and should not be relied on.

## 2. Free field solvers / extractors: what each computes, licence, headless use, validation

### Takeaway
For this board class the realistic free stack is PyPEEC (R + self/mutual L from Gerber, pure Python, MPL-2.0) or FastHenry2 via subprocess for inductance, FasterCap (LGPL) for capacitance, padne (GPL-3.0, CLI `solve`) for DC IR drop, and openEMS/gerber2ems (Apache-2.0 wrapper) only as an out-of-class reference; no single open tool produces a full RLC post-layout model, and published quantitative validation is thin for all of them.

### Cited Findings
- PyPEEC: 3D quasi-magnetostatic solver, FFT-accelerated PEEC with voxelization; pure Python on NumPy/SciPy/Joblib/Rasterio/Shapely/Pillow/Matplotlib/PyVista; multi-core CPU and GPU (CuPy/CUDA); optional MKL/PARDISO, PyAMG, FFTW. Mesher accepts STL, PNG and GERBER; static and frequency-domain solves. JOSS paper dated 02 September 2025. [S] — [JOSS 10.21105/joss.06644](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.06644); [PyPI pypeec](https://pypi.org/project/pypeec)
- PyPEEC stated limits: "no capacitive effects, no dielectric domains, no advanced boundary conditions, no model order reduction", voxel geometries only. PyPI shows a 5.x release line (5.2.2 listed), i.e. actively maintained. [S] — [PyPI pypeec](https://pypi.org/project/pypeec); [PyPI pypeec 5.2.2](https://pypi.org/project/pypeec/5.2.2)
- FasterCap: source under LGPL; available for Windows and Linux 32/64-bit in binary and source; building needs FastFieldSolvers' sibling `Geometry` and `LinAlgebra` libraries at the same directory level. [S] — [FastFieldSolvers FasterCap](https://fastfieldsolvers.com/fastercap.htm); [FastFieldSolvers software](https://fastfieldsolvers.com/software.htm)
- FastCap2: the WRCad (Whiteley Research) branch "compiles for Linux 32/64 bits as well as for Windows in shell only mode"; the FastFieldSolvers branch is the Windows GUI port. [S] — [FastFieldSolvers downloads](https://www.fastfieldsolvers.com/dwnld02.htm)
- FastHenry2 headless on Linux: third-party project pyCoilGen documents cloning FastHenry2 from GitHub, running `make` in `src`, and pointing a `fasthenry_bin` config parameter at the binary — a subprocess integration pattern. [S] — [pyCoilGen installation doc](https://github.com/kev-m/pyCoilGen/blob/58486cf834f9bcb8f4ce73e2bafbcc9eff004a76/docs/source/installation.md)
- FreeCAD EM Workbench (LGPL 2.1) generates FastHenry/FasterCap input and exposes a Python EM API, but that API requires FreeCAD. [S] — [FreeCAD wiki, EM Workbench](https://wiki.freecad.org/EM_Workbench)
- padne: GPL-3.0; "2.5D finite element solver for Laplace's equation"; computes DC voltage drop and current density from a KiCad project; sources/sinks declared by schematic text directives `!padne VOLTAGE|CURRENT|RESISTANCE|REGULATOR|COPPER key=value` with endpoints as `DESIGNATOR.PAD`; copper conductivity from the board stackup or a `COPPER conductivity=` override. CLI: `padne gui`, `padne solve project.kicad_pro out.padne`, `padne show`, `padne paraview out.padne dir/` (one `.vtu` per layer). Needs KiCad's Python bindings; Linux only shown (Ubuntu steps, `padne-linux-x64` binary); 457 commits, no tagged releases, no accuracy claims in the README. [F] — [github.com/atx/padne](https://github.com/atx/padne)
- padne received an NLnet NGI0 Commons Fund grant starting 2026-04 whose goals include validating accuracy by fabricating and measuring test PCBs, parallelisation, and documentation — i.e. measurement validation was still future work at that date. nixpkgs lists version 0.3. [S] — [NLnet project page](https://nlnet.nl/project/Padne/); [nixpkgs padne](https://mynixos.com/nixpkgs/package/python313Packages.padne); [KiCad forum announcement](https://forum.kicad.info/t/presenting-padne-a-tool-for-dc-power-delivery-network-analysis/64966)
- OpenROAD PDNSim is an open static IR-drop tool but targets IC power grids, not PCBs. [S] — [PDNSim documentation](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_downloads/86b37e131fb3a938455f51249d86af88/PDNSim-documentation.pdf)
- kicad-happy (aklofas) ships an `extract_parasitics.py` step that derives trace resistance and via inductance from PCB geometry and feeds a parasitics file to `simulate_subcircuits.py` for ngspice/LTspice/Xyce testbenches; its own example: "48 mΩ trace resistance shifts RC filter fc down 0.3%"; its docs say it is worth using for >100 kΩ feedback networks, LC filters/RF matching, long analog traces, and that for typical digital power designs the ideal simulation is usually enough. Licence not stated in the retrieved pages. [S] — [kicad-happy spice skill](https://skills.sh/aklofas/kicad-happy/spice)

### Inferences
- Licence/AGPL-3.0 reading [recall of licence terms, not re-verified]: Apache-2.0 (gerber2ems) and LGPL (FasterCap, EM Workbench) code can be combined into an AGPL-3.0 work; MPL-2.0 (PyPEEC) is importable as a library with its files staying MPL; GPL-3.0 (padne, and openEMS which is GPL) is combinable with AGPL-3.0 under each licence's section 13 and is in any case unproblematic when invoked as a separate process exchanging files. FastHenry2's and FastCap2's exact licence text was NOT confirmed — read the LICENSE in each source tree before bundling into the Docker image.
- For a 40-part 2-layer board the only extractor that is import-and-call from Python 3.11 with no compiled external binary is PyPEEC; everything else is "write input file → subprocess → parse output".
- padne's `solve` subcommand is the natural headless entry, but it consumes a `.kicad_pro` plus schematic directives; Circuit OS would have to either emit a KiCad project from its `Board` IR or reimplement the (simple) 2.5D Laplace solve itself. Given that a rasterised finite-difference sheet-resistance solve is a few dozen lines of SciPy, reimplementation is likely cheaper than the KiCad dependency.
- Runtime order of magnitude [engineering estimate, unverified]: closed-form pass over all tracks — milliseconds; rasterised DC resistive mesh at 0.1 mm pixels on a 50 × 50 mm board (≈ 250 k unknowns per layer, sparse SPD solve) — about a second; PEEC voxel solve of the same board — tens of seconds to minutes depending on voxel pitch; openEMS FDTD per net — minutes to hours. None of these figures has a published source.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark of PyPEEC against FastHenry or against measurement was found, and no runtime figures for Gerber-derived boards. The PyPEEC documentation site (pypeec.otvam.ch) and the JOSS paper body were not opened.
- Not researched at all: Elmer FEM, Palace (AWS), FEniCS/scikit-fem 2D cross-section solvers, scuff-em, Sparselizard, GetDP/Gmsh, OpenParEM, atlc, pcbmodelgen, pyems, "SI-wrapper", "kicad-to-openEMS", "pcb-rf-tools", "kicad-pdn", voltage-drop plugins. Their status, licences and maintenance are unknown from this session.
- A project literally named "kicad-parasitics" was not found by search; the closest hit was kicad-happy's extractor. The earlier report's description of kicad-parasitics could not be re-confirmed or attributed to a repository URL.
- No official Python wrapper for FastHenry2/FastCap2/FasterCap was found; "pyfasthenry" was not located.

## 3. Antmicro gerber2ems

### Takeaway
gerber2ems is an Apache-2.0 Python CLI that rasterises Gerbers plus a stackup JSON into an openEMS FDTD model and returns per-trace S-parameters and impedance; it is built for multi-GHz digital interfaces, gives no quantitative accuracy or runtime claims, and is out of class for < 10 MHz boards.

### Cited Findings
- Licence Apache-2.0, "Copyright (c) 2023-2026 Antmicro"; 217 commits; no tagged releases. [F] — [github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems)
- Inputs in a `fab/` folder: one Gerber per simulated copper layer named `<text>-<stackup layer name>.gbr`; `stackup.json` (layer name, type, thickness, material, epsilon, lossTangent, `format_version`); Excellon PTH drill file `*-PTH.drl`; position file `*-pos.csv` in which port designators begin with `SP`; optional `netinfo.json`; plus `simulation.json` (frequency start/stop in Hz, `max_steps`, `pixel_size`, via plating thickness and fill epsilon, grid pitches, margins, port width/length/impedance/layer/plane/excite, traces and differential pairs). [F] — [gerber2ems README](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems)
- Dependencies: openEMS (commit `a30587728affa4f8451e13819981899bd8ab6b64` recommended as the last tested), gerbv, python3, optional ParaView; a Dockerfile is provided (`docker build -t gerber2ems .`). [F] — [gerber2ems README](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems)
- CLI: `gerber2ems -a` (all), `-g` geometry, `-s` simulate, `-p` post-process; `--export-field`; helpers `ems2paraview`, `ems2png`. Outputs: CSV S-parameter and impedance data, S-parameter plots, Smith chart of S11 per excitation, impedance plot per port. [F] — [gerber2ems README](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems)
- Stated limitations: capacitors are not simulated; every terminated trace or pour needs a port or a ground connection; large boards need a larger `pixel_size` (libcairo limit); field exports can take "hundreds GB"; grid pitch must satisfy optimal ≤ diagonal ≤ perpendicular ≤ max ≤ λmin/10. [F] — [gerber2ems README](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems)
- Validation: the `stub_short` example ships a `vna.csv` and a comparison plot of simulated vs VNA impedance, taken from slices of Antmicro's open Signal Integrity Test Board, but no numeric error figure is given. No total runtime is stated (a sample log shows ~294 MC/s and a 10000-step cap, illustrative only). [F] — [gerber2ems README](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems)
- Flow per Antmicro: select nets, build simulation cases, export PCB slices, simulate each trace or differential pair separately with ports at the trace ends; 2025 update added trace-delay simulation and ParaView export. [S] — [Antmicro blog 2023-11](https://antmicro.com/blog/2023/11/open-source-signal-integrity-analysis); [Antmicro blog 2024-07](https://antmicro.com/blog/2024/07/automated-pcb-trace-selection-for-si-simulation); [Antmicro blog 2025-07](https://antmicro.com/blog/2025/07/recent-improvements-to-antmicros-signal-integrity-simulation-flow)

### Inferences
- Not worth integrating for Circuit OS's class. [calc] At 10 MHz the guided wavelength on FR-4 microstrip (εeff ≈ 3) is ≈ 17 m, so a 100 mm board is ≈ λ/170; even at a 100 MHz edge-knee frequency it is ≈ λ/17. A full-wave S-parameter result would be indistinguishable from the lumped L/C of section 1 while costing minutes-to-hours per net and requiring port footprints (`SP*`) the templates do not have.
- Its real value here is as a methodology reference: the fabrication-file-in / JSON-config / CSV-out contract and the practice of shipping VNA data beside each example are worth copying for Circuit OS's own extractor regression suite.
- Because "capacitors are not simulated", gerber2ems cannot do PDN/decoupling analysis even in principle.

### Gaps
- No published quantitative accuracy (e.g. Ω or dB error vs VNA) and no wall-clock runtime per trace were found.
- Whether it runs on a 2-layer 1.6 mm board without modification was not tested or documented in what I read.

## 4. PDN / power integrity with free tools, and deterministic decoupling rules

### Takeaway
DC IR drop is solvable for free (padne, or a self-written resistive mesh), but for these boards the decision-relevant PI check is geometric: a vendor-published "decoupling capacitor trace ≤ 6 mm (0.25 in) from the pin" rule exists and maps, by back-of-envelope, to roughly 10 nH of loop inductance — adequate for a target impedance of a few ohms up to tens of MHz.

### Cited Findings
- Microchip device documentation: decoupling capacitors "as close to the pins as possible"; if space forces the capacitor to the opposite side through a via, "ensure that the trace length from the pin to the capacitor is within one-quarter inch (6 mm)" (other revisions: "no greater than 0.25 inch (6 mm)"). [S] — [Microchip online docs, Decoupling Capacitors](https://onlinedocs.microchip.com/oxy/GUID-7D545F23-E6CC-4AB1-9E35-ED2CB0ADA187-en-US-3/GUID-FD97F122-F9A6-43B5-B877-2E574000E001.html)
- A forum-level figure that ECAD loop-inductance reports for decoupling capacitors typically aim for "2 nH and lower", with plane-pair spacing a first-order effect (weak source; applies to multilayer boards with plane pairs). [S] — [edaboard thread](https://edaboard.com/threads/decoupling-and-bulk-capacitor.355570)
- padne computes DC drop and current density by 2.5D FEM and models a `REGULATOR` as a current-controlled current source (example: 12 V→5 V at 80% efficiency, gain ≈ 0.52); multi-pad terminals are star-joined through 1 mΩ coupling resistors by default. [F] — [github.com/atx/padne](https://github.com/atx/padne)
- KiCad community discussion of open power-analysis options points to FreeCAD + fcad_pcb export into an FEM tool as the manual route before padne existed. [S] — [KiCad forum, Open source power analysis tool?](https://forum.kicad.info/t/open-source-power-analysis-tool/21652)

### Inferences
- [calc, target-impedance method recalled: Ztarget = ΔV/ΔI] 3.3 V rail, 5% ripple budget = 165 mV. ATmega-class load step ≈ 50 mA → Ztarget ≈ 3.3 Ω; ESP32 radio burst ≈ 300 mA → ≈ 0.55 Ω. A decoupling loop of L nH exceeds Ztarget at f = Ztarget/(2πL): 10 nH → 52 MHz (3.3 Ω) or 8.8 MHz (0.55 Ω); 30 nH → 17 MHz or 2.9 MHz.
- [calc, using ≈ 0.8 nH/mm for a narrow trace over a 1.6 mm-distant pour from section 1] Microchip's 6 mm limit each way ≈ 12 mm of loop path ≈ 10 nH plus ≈ 1.2 nH per via. So "6 mm" ≈ "10–12 nH", which is acceptable for 8–16 MHz MCUs (first few clock harmonics below 50 MHz) and marginal for an ESP32 burst — consistent with ESP32 modules carrying their own on-module decoupling.
- Defensible deterministic rules that follow: (R1) every IC supply pin has a ≥ 100 nF capacitor whose pad-to-pin routed length ≤ 6 mm [vendor-sourced threshold]; (R2) total routed loop length VDD-pin → cap → GND-pin ≤ ~15 mm, equivalently estimated loop L ≤ 15 nH [derived, not sourced]; (R3) the capacitor's GND return reaches the IC GND pin by pour or a trace of comparable length, not via a detour [derived]; (R4) at most one via in each leg of the loop [derived from 1.2 nH/via]. R1 is citable; R2–R4 are engineering derivations and must be labelled as such in the product.
- DC IR drop is almost never binding here: [calc] 300 mA through 50 mm of 0.5 mm trace (100 squares ≈ 49 mΩ) drops 15 mV (0.45% of 3.3 V); the 70 mA relay coil through 30 mm of 0.25 mm trace (≈ 59 mΩ) drops 4 mV. A closed-form per-net squares count with a 2% drop threshold replaces a mesh solver for these boards; the mesh solver only earns its keep when power flows through pours with neck-downs.
- MLCC DC-bias derating matters more than any layout parasitic for the bulk capacitance value: [recall] X5R/X7R parts in small cases can lose a large fraction of nominal capacitance near rated voltage. This belongs in the tolerance box as a one-sided interval on C, not in the layout extractor.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Larry Smith / Istvan Novak target-impedance primary papers; Ott's and Bogatin's 2-layer decoupling guidance; ST AN2586/AN4488 and Microchip AVR042 numeric recommendations (the search specifically failed to surface them); any published measurement of PDN impedance vs capacitor distance on 2-layer boards.
- Capacitor model sources (Murata SimSurfing, KEMET K-SIM, TDK SEAT): availability of SPICE/S-parameter models and, critically, their redistribution licence terms were not researched. Do not bundle vendor models without reading the terms.
- No citable formula for capacitor mounting inductance was retrieved; the 0.8–1 nH/mm figure is a derived/rule-of-thumb value.
- No open "kicad IR drop" plugin other than padne was identified.

## 5. Signal-integrity residue for low-speed 2-layer boards and what open tools can check

### Takeaway
The only quantitatively sourced SI rule recovered is TI's RS-485 stub bound (stub round-trip delay under one tenth of driver rise time); KiCad's custom-rule language expresses minimum-separation and clearance conditions by netclass/area but there is no evidence it can express a "pad X must be within N mm of pad Y" maximum-distance rule, so proximity, loop-area and return-path checks have to be implemented in Circuit OS's own Board IR.

### Cited Findings
- RS-485 stub rule: L_stub ≤ 0.1 · tr · v · c, with tr the driver 10–90% rise time, c = 3e8 m/s and v the cable/trace velocity factor. [S] — [TI THVD1428 datasheet, Stub Length](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/THVD1428/datasheet/stub-length-sllsei9862); guidance originates in [TI SLLA272D, The RS-485 Design Guide](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SLLA272D/GUID-D689C67D-ED23-477F-BC1E-F680139458AB)
- SLLA272 assumes ≈ 78% velocity factor cable and tabulates maximum stub lengths per driver rise time; the search summary reports 7 ft for a 100 ns driver (SN65HVD12). [S] — [TI SLLA272D](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SLLA272D/GUID-D689C67D-ED23-477F-BC1E-F680139458AB)
- KiCad custom rules: `physical_clearance` constraints conditioned on net class, e.g. `(constraint physical_clearance (min 1.5mm))` with a condition such as `A.NetClass == 'HV' || B.NetClass == 'HV'`. [S] — [KiCad forum, custom DRC distance rule](https://forum.kicad.info/t/custom-drc-distance-rule-with-exception/47860); [third-party mirror of KiCad design-rules docs](https://www.mintlify.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/workflow/design-rules)
- KiCad rule semantics reported by users: rules are evaluated last-to-first and the first match wins; rules "can't have knowledge of" connected items (e.g. a via rule cannot depend on the attached track width) — a Python DRC interface was suggested as the better route. [S] — [KiCad forum, ECSS granular rules](https://forum.kicad.info/t/custom-drc-rules-more-granular-control-for-ecss-specifications/41686); [KiCad forum, rule override question](https://forum.kicad.info/t/why-do-my-custom-design-rules-override-the-minimum-clearance-from-the-constraints-section/58834)
- A KiCad forum thread exists specifically asking how to write a custom rule checking object proximity. [S] — [KiCad forum, Custom design rule to check object proximity](https://forum.kicad.info/t/custom-design-rule-to-check-object-proximity/54246)
- Custom `physical_clearance` / `physical_hole_clearance` rules are reported as not honoured by the interactive router, though DRC flags them. [S] — [KiCad GitLab work item 24132](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/work_items/24132)

### Inferences
- [calc from the TI formula] For a fast non-slew-limited transceiver with tr ≈ 30 ns: L_stub ≤ 0.1 × 30e-9 × 0.78 × 3e8 ≈ 0.70 m in cable; on FR-4 (v ≈ 0.55) ≈ 0.5 m. The on-board distance from MAX485 pins to the connector (tens of mm) is ≥ 10× inside the bound, so the on-board RS-485 stub check is a provable pass and the rule is only meaningful for the external cable topology — which is outside the layout.
- [calc] DHT22 one-wire rise time is set by cable capacitance, not the board: 4.7 kΩ × (≈ 100 pF/m × length). At 20 m, τ ≈ 9.4 µs and 10–90% rise ≈ 21 µs; board trace capacitance (≈ 2 pF) contributes 0.1%. The meaningful back-annotation for this template is therefore a **cable** capacitance interval in the netlist (already a template parameter), not PCB extraction. [recall] DHT22 distinguishes bits by high times of roughly 26–28 µs vs 70 µs, which is why ≈ 20 m at 4.7 kΩ is the edge; verify against the datasheet before encoding a limit.
- Lumped-validity check [calc]: a 100 mm trace has ≈ 0.57 ns one-way delay. With a 5 ns edge the ratio is 0.11 (lumped treatment fine); with 1–2 ns edges (ESP32/STM32 GPIO at high drive, unverified figures) it rises to 0.3–0.6, where ringing on unterminated lines becomes plausible. A deterministic rule "trace delay ≤ tr/6, else require series termination or reduced drive strength" covers this without a solver.
- Checks that need only the Board IR and are implementable as geometry: (a) return-path continuity — project each F.Cu track polyline onto B.Cu and flag any segment over a pour gap/slot or with no pour beneath it; (b) loop area — polygon area enclosed by a signal track and its nearest return route, per net; (c) parallel-run length between an aggressor class (relay coil, clock, RS-485 driver side) and a victim class (analog reference, reset, crystal) closer than k·h; (d) crystal keep-out — no foreign tracks within the bounding box of crystal + load caps, load-cap-to-pin routed length limit; (e) flyback diode within N mm of the relay coil pads and the loop coil→diode→coil area bounded; (f) decoupling proximity (section 4). All thresholds other than the vendor-cited ones must be labelled heuristic.
- Because KiCad rule conditions are pairwise A/B predicates with min/max constraint values on clearance-type quantities, and users report no access to connectivity context, "max distance between two specific pads" is at best awkward; the forum thread asking for exactly this suggests it is not a first-class feature. Treat as unsupported unless the official docs prove otherwise.

## 6. Thermal and trace/via current capacity

### Takeaway
IPC-2221's closed form is the citable conservative floor; IPC-2152 is chart-based with no official equation, and third-party curve fits disagree on how much margin it releases — for currents of a few hundred mA the IPC-2221 formula already passes any sane trace, so board-level thermal simulation is not warranted for this class.

### Cited Findings
- IPC-2221: I = k · ΔT^0.44 · A^0.725, A in mil², k = 0.048 external / 0.024 internal. It ignores the cooling effect of nearby pours and planes and so over-sizes traces. [S] — [Altium IPC-2221 calculator](https://resources.altium.com/p/ipc-2221-calculator-pcb-trace-current-and-heating)
- IPC-2152 (2009) is chart/nomograph based — an "unadjusted" cross-section for a given current, then corrections for nearby plane, board thickness and dielectric thermal conductivity. [S] — [Cadence, IPC-2152 vs IPC-2221](https://resources.system-analysis.cadence.com/blog/ipc-2152-vs-ipc-2221-which-to-use-for-pcb-thermal-analysis)
- A third-party curve fit of IPC-2152 is quoted as I ≈ k · ΔT^0.5 · A^0.65 with k ≈ 0.089 external / 0.063 internal, claiming 1.5–2× the IPC-2221 current at equal temperature rise; another secondary source instead says IPC-2152 allows 15–25% narrower traces. These conflict in magnitude and neither is the standard's own equation. [S] — [Salitronic trace width calculator](https://salitronic.com/pcb-trace-width-calculator/); [PCBSync article](https://pcbsync.com/?p=34352)
- Secondary sources note the IPC-2152 benefit is conditional on adjacent copper actually sinking heat; without it the narrower trace runs as hot as IPC-2221 predicted. [S] — [PCBSync article](https://pcbsync.com/?p=34352); see also [Siemens blog on thermal influence on current capacity](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/simulating-the-real-world/2021/01/20/thermal-influence-on-maximum-current-carrying-capacity/)

### Inferences
- [calc from the cited IPC-2221 formula] A 0.25 mm × 35 µm external trace has A ≈ 13.6 mil²; at ΔT = 10 °C, I ≈ 0.048 × 2.75 × 6.63 ≈ 0.88 A. The largest template current (a few hundred mA; relay coil 70 mA) is 3–12× below the limit at the narrowest plausible trace. The check is therefore a closed-form assertion, and the result can be stated as a proof obligation "I_max(net) ≤ I_IPC2221(width, ΔT=10 °C)".
- The one case that is not negligible is the **relay contact path when switching mains loads**: a 5–10 A contact current needs ≈ 2.5–7 mm of 1 oz external trace by the same formula at ΔT = 10 °C (to be computed per design), and that net never appears in the low-voltage SPICE netlist. It must be covered by a separate rule keyed on the relay's rated contact current.
- Use IPC-2221 (conservative) as the pass/fail bound and expose IPC-2152 only as informational, because no official closed form exists to cite and the fits disagree.
- Board-level thermal FEM is not justified: total dissipation of these boards is well under 1 W with the exception of a linear regulator, which is better handled by a θJA datasheet calculation in the circuit layer.

### Gaps
- No free Python implementation of IPC-2152 ("ipc2152" package) was confirmed to exist; KiCad calculator's track-width formula basis was not verified this session; Saturn PCB Toolkit's licence status was not re-checked.
- Via current capacity formulas/sources not retrieved.
- Free thermal options (Elmer, OpenFOAM, KiCad thermal proposals) were not researched; the earlier report's "no free board-level thermal solver" finding stands unrefuted but also unre-examined.

## 7. Creepage and clearance for a mains-switching relay

### Takeaway
For 250 V working voltage, pollution degree 2, material group IIIa/b, the commonly cited basic-insulation creepage is 2.5 mm (from the IEC 60950-1 table, the predecessor of IEC 62368-1), while IPC-2221B Table 6-1 gives only 1.25 mm (uncoated external) or 0.4 mm (coated) and is not a safety standard; KiCad 9 has a `creepage` custom-rule constraint, but user reports through 9.0.3 show missed violations near slots, run-to-run inconsistency and severe slowdowns, so it cannot be the sole safety gate.

### Cited Findings
- IPC-2221B Table 6-1, 151–300 V rows (151–170, 171–250, 251–300 V): B2 external uncoated = 1.25 mm; B4 external with permanent polymer coating = 0.4 mm. Voltages are peak AC or DC between conductors; B2 applies sea level to 3050 m; solder resist is reported as not counting as the B4 coating. [S] — [Sierra Circuits, IPC-2221 circuit board design](https://protoexpress.com/blog/ipc-2221-circuit-board-design); [EMA, clearance and creepage table](https://www.ema-eda.com/ema-resources/blog/pcb-clearance-and-creepage-distance-table/)
- Some secondary tables mislabel the IPC-2221B columns (1.25 mm shown as "bare board external", 0.4 mm as "conformal coated"). [S] — [AtlasPCB blog](https://atlaspcb.com/blog/pcb-creepage-clearance-ipc-2221b-high-voltage-spacing)
- "The Basic Insulation creepage distance required for a Pollution Degree 2, Material Group IIIa/b … 250 VRMS system is 2.5 mm" (table sourced from IEC 60950-1). [S] — [Skyworks AN583](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/SkyWorks/SL/documents/public/application-notes/AN583.pdf)
- Analog Devices' CTI article gives the same 2.5 mm at 250 V, pollution degree 2, material group III on the IEC 60950 basis, and relates comparative tracking index to material group and allowable working voltage. [S] — [Analog Devices, The Relation Between the Comparative Tracking Index and Working Voltage](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/technical-articles/comparative-tracking-index-and-working-voltage.html)
- One vendor blog gives ≈ 4 mm for material group III at 250 V RMS, conflicting with the two semiconductor-vendor tables above; treated as unreliable. [S] — [AllPCB creepage guide](https://www.allpcb.com/blog/pcb-knowledge/creepage-distance-your-comprehensive-guide-for-pcb-design-safety.html)
- KiCad 9.0 introduced creepage custom rules; minimal syntax `(rule creepage (constraint creepage (min 5mm)))`; conditional example `(condition "A.hasNetclass('BI') && !B.hasNetclass('BI')")` with a 20 mm creepage. Creepage was behind an advanced-config flag during development and is described as enabled by default at release. Rules live in the project's `.kicad_dru` file. [S] — [KiCad forum, How do I use the new creepage custom rules? (v9.0.0)](https://forum.kicad.info/t/how-do-i-use-the-new-creepage-custom-rules-v9-0-0/59328)
- Reported defects: creepage errors found on first DRC run and absent on re-run; a violation near a slot not reported even with a 10 mm rule, with detection flipping when a trace is nudged. [S] — [KiCad forum, Creepage DRC does not work](https://forum.kicad.info/t/creepage-drc-does-not-work/59857)
- Performance: on 9.0.3 a board whose DRC took ~30 s ran 5–10 minutes and then crashed with creepage checking enabled on copper-dense boards. [S] — [KiCad GitLab issue 21482](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/issues/21482)
- A user reports `enclosedByArea`-conditioned rules affecting clearance but not creepage (unanswered in the retrieved material). [S] — [KiCad forum, custom DRC rule creepage enclosedByArea](https://forum.kicad.info/t/custom-drc-rule-creepage-enclosedbyarea/67126)
- KiCad's source tree contains a dedicated creepage DRC unit test. [S] — [KiCad doxygen, test_drc_creepage.cpp](https://docs.kicad.org/doxygen/test__drc__creepage_8cpp_source.html)

### Inferences
- The gap between IPC-2221B (1.25 mm) and the safety-standard figure (2.5 mm basic) is 2×; a tool that checks only IPC-2221B spacing on a mains relay board would pass layouts that fail safety creepage. The mains template should gate on the safety-standard value.
- [recall, unverified] Mains-to-SELV (user-touchable low-voltage side, i.e. the MCU side of the relay) normally requires reinforced/double insulation, conventionally twice the basic creepage → ≈ 5.0 mm at 250 V, PD2, MG IIIa; and standard FR-4 is conventionally assigned CTI 175–249 = material group IIIa. Both statements need confirmation against IEC 62368-1/60664-1 text before being encoded; if confirmed, the rule set is: contact-to-contact (functional) per IPC-2221B/functional table; contact-net to coil/logic nets ≥ reinforced creepage, with a routed slot permitted to lengthen the path.
- Since Circuit OS owns its Board IR, the robust approach is an in-house creepage computation (shortest path along the board surface between copper of the two net groups, treating slots/edges as obstacles the path must go around) with KiCad's creepage DRC used only as a cross-check — the reported KiCad instability argues against depending on it.
- Product-level honesty: the tool can state "meets the tabulated distance X for stated assumptions (250 V RMS, PD2, MG IIIa, altitude ≤ 2000 m, overvoltage category II)"; it cannot claim compliance with a safety standard.

## 8. Back-annotation mechanics: getting extracted R/L/C into SPICE and into the tolerance proofs

### Takeaway
ngspice has everything needed for lumped back-annotation (series R/L, shunt C, pairwise K coupling) and three lossy-line models, of which the multiconductor CPL model is undocumented with known memory leaks; no open PCB flow that emits a complete post-layout RLC netlist automatically was confirmed — kicad-happy's R + via-L injection into testbenches is the closest — so Circuit OS must write its own annotator, and for this class the annotated values are best carried as conservative intervals rather than point values.

### Cited Findings
- ngspice coupled inductors: `K12 L1 L2 0.99` form; 0 < K ≤ 1; multiple inductors are coupled pairwise and ngspice checks the coupling matrix, warning on physically impossible combinations. Listed as "Coupled (Mutual) Inductors", section 3.2.11 in the ngspice 27/30 manuals. [S] — [ngspice 30 manual (mirror)](https://ftp.unpad.ac.id/macports/distfiles/ngspice/ngspice-30-manual.pdf); quoted in [KiCad forum thread](https://forum.kicad.info/t/simulate-synchronous-active-rectifier-with-mosfet/22713)
- ngspice device inventory: TRA (lossless line), LTRA (lossy line, "original Spice model", no parallel-code switches), TXL ("simple lossy transmission line", from KSPICE), CPL ("simple coupled multiconductor line", from SWEC/KSPICE, "not documented", memory leaks listed as a known issue), URC (uniform distributed RC). [S] — [ngspice DEVICES file (Debian source)](https://sources.debian.org/src/ngspice/24-1/DEVICES)
- kicad-happy's flow: extract parasitics from PCB geometry → parasitics file → testbench generator inserts trace resistance and via inductance between components → ngspice/LTspice/Xyce; reported sensitivity example 48 mΩ → −0.3% RC cutoff. [S] — [kicad-happy spice skill](https://skills.sh/aklofas/kicad-happy/spice)
- padne emits per-layer `.vtu` fields and a `.padne` solution file, not a SPICE netlist. [F] — [github.com/atx/padne](https://github.com/atx/padne)

### Inferences
- **Netlist transformation (design recommendation).** Split each routed net at its pads into a star/tree of segments; for each segment between two pads emit `R<seg>` (squares × sheet R) in series with `L<seg>` (length × per-length loop L over the pour, or partial L if no pour), and at each pad node a shunt `C<seg>` to the reference node (length × per-length C, split half-half, π-model). Vias add `R ≈ 1 mΩ` and `L ≈ 1.2 nH` in series. Mutual coupling between two parallel segments, only if flagged by the geometric aggressor/victim rule, as a `K` between their `L` elements. Net names of the original netlist are preserved by renaming the pad-side nodes (`net#pad`) so that graded probes stay attached to the component pin they were defined on.
- **Ladder sizing.** [recall of the standard λ/10–λ/20 rule] One π-section per segment is valid while segment length ≤ λ/10 at the highest frequency of interest. [calc] At a 100 MHz knee on FR-4 λ/10 ≈ 174 mm, so a single section per segment is always sufficient on these boards; LTRA/TXL/CPL are unnecessary and CPL in particular should be avoided given the cited "not documented / memory leaks" status.
- **IC-flow analogy.** [recall] IC sign-off extracts parasitics to SPEF/DSPF and re-simulates across RC corners (Cmax/Cmin/RCmax/RCmin). The PCB analogue is exactly two corners per parasitic — lower bound (zero, i.e. the ideal schematic) and a conservative upper bound — which is precisely an interval.
- **Entering the tolerance-box proof.** Because the existing prover does exact rational nodal analysis over a box, each parasitic can be introduced as an additional symbolic parameter p ∈ [0, p_max] with rational endpoints, where p_max = closed-form value × (1 + margin) and the margin covers (i) geometry/etch tolerance, (ii) εr and thickness spread, (iii) formula model error, (iv) temperature (copper R rises with T). Setting the lower bound to 0 makes the ideal circuit a member of the box, so the post-layout proof strictly generalises the pre-layout one. For DC-graded quantities (divider ratio, LED current, bias voltages, RS-485 fail-safe bias levels) only R parasitics enter and the system stays rational → z3 handles it unchanged. For frequency/time-graded quantities (RC cutoff, rise time) C parasitics add to an existing C term (still rational in s); series L raises the order and is best dismissed by a separate bound (ωL ≪ R at the graded frequency) rather than carried into z3.
- **Cheap pre-filter to avoid growing the proof.** Before adding a parasitic parameter, test a sufficient negligibility condition: if p_max/X ≤ ε for the component X it is in series/parallel with, and ε is ≤ 1/10 of the remaining slack between the proved worst case and the 15% grading limit, record "negligible by bound" with the numbers and skip it. With trace R at 1.6–3.5 orders below 1% tolerance (prior finding), nearly every parasitic on these boards exits here; the value of the feature is the recorded proof that it is negligible, plus the rare flagged exception (very high-impedance nodes ≥ 100 kΩ–1 MΩ where a few pF matters; kicad-happy's docs independently name ">100 kΩ feedback networks" as the case worth extracting).
- **Bounding solver error.** If a field solver (PyPEEC/FastHenry/FasterCap) is used instead of a closed form, its output is a float with discretisation error and cannot be a proof premise by itself; use it only to tighten the interval's centre while keeping the closed-form conservative upper bound as the proved bound, or run a mesh-refinement pair and inflate by the observed difference (heuristic, not a guarantee).

### Gaps
- No open tool named "pcb2spice" / "KiCad PCB to SPICE" producing a full RLC post-layout netlist was confirmed; Qucs-S capabilities in this area were not researched.
- ngspice manual text for LTRA/TXL/CPL parameters (R, L, G, C, LEN; coupled matrices) was not read directly; only the DEVICES inventory was retrieved, and that copy is from an old ngspice release (Debian source "24-1") — CPL status in current ngspice (45+) should be re-checked.

### Inferences

Acceptance: R within 1–2% (pure geometry), L and C within ~10% of closed form, and always inside the declared interval.
- Bench validation needs, sized to the magnitudes computed above: trace R of 10–100 mΩ → 4-wire (Kelvin) milliohm measurement, e.g. a bench supply forcing 1 A and a DMM reading mV; trace/pad C of 1–10 pF and L of 10–100 nH → an LCR meter with 100 kHz–1 MHz test frequency and careful open/short compensation, since fixture parasitics are the same order as the quantity; PDN impedance below ~1 Ω → 2-port shunt-through measurement on a VNA. [recall] A NanoVNA-class instrument has limited dynamic range for sub-ohm shunt-through work and is realistically useful for decade-level sanity checks of resonance frequencies rather than milliohm-accurate PDN curves; this limit was not sourced.
- Given how small every parasitic is relative to tolerance on these boards, measurement validation is needed to defend the **upper bounds**, not point accuracy: the test is "measured value ≤ declared p_max" on a small set of fabricated coupons.

## 10. EMC: what is defensible to check in software, and what is out of scope

### Takeaway
The two small-source formulas (differential-mode loop: E ∝ f²·A·I/r; common-mode cable: E ∝ f·L·I/r) are confirmed by vendor app notes and give usable order-of-magnitude budgets — a few cm² of clock-current loop area and roughly 5 µA of common-mode cable current — but they are estimates with known large error near resonances, so software can enforce loop-area and cable-entry rules and must declare emissions compliance out of scope.

### Cited Findings
- Differential-mode (small loop, over a ground plane): E[µV/m] = 2.6 · A[cm²] · I · f[MHz]² / R — equivalent to 2.63e-14 · f² · A · I / r in SI; common-mode cable form uses f in Hz and is equivalent to 1.26e-6 · f · L · I / r. [S] — [NXP/Freescale AN1050](https://NXP.com/docs/en/application-note/AN1050.pdf)
- Practical common-mode form E = 0.4 · f[MHz] · L[m] · I[µA] µV/m at 3 m; the same note uses 100 µV/m at 3 m as the FCC Class B reference level. [S] — [Digi/Rabbit TN221](https://hub.digi.com/dp/path=/support/asset/technical-note-tn221-pc-board-layout-suggestions-to-reduce-emi-and-rf-emissions-with-the-rabbit-3000-microprocessor/)
- For equal currents common-mode radiation far exceeds differential-mode; DM rises with f², CM with f. [S] — [ROHM TechWeb, switching noise / EMC basics](https://techweb.rohm.com/know-how/nowisee/6899/)
- LearnEMC's worked example states a 5 cm × 1 cm loop would need about 1.7 A at 100 MHz to reach the Class B limit, which does not match what the DM formula above yields (a few mA); unresolved conflict — possibly different assumptions (limit distance, units, or a different model). [S] — [LearnEMC Question of the Week, 2024-07-22](https://learnemc.com/qotw-240722)

### Inferences
- The brief quoted a DM coefficient of 1.316e-14; the sourced app-note form corresponds to 2.63e-14, exactly 2× larger. [recall] The factor of two is the usual allowance for ground-plane reflection in an open-area test site (free-space vs over-ground form). Use 2.63e-14 for limit comparisons, since test sites include the reflection.
- [calc with the cited CM formula, 100 µV/m at 3 m] Allowed common-mode current on a 1 m cable at 50 MHz: I = E·r/(1.257e-6·f·L) ≈ 4.8 µA. This is the dominant EMC risk for the DHT22-cable and RS-485 templates, and it cannot be predicted from layout (it depends on ground-noise voltage driving the cable and on the cable/enclosure geometry). The defensible software checks are structural: cable signals referenced to an unbroken pour at the connector, no high-di/dt loop adjacent to the connector, optional common-mode choke/TVS footprints present.
- [calc with the cited DM formula] 16 MHz clock, 5th harmonic at 80 MHz, assumed 5 mA harmonic current: A_max = E·r/(2.63e-14·f²·I) ≈ 3.6 cm². Scaling: area ∝ 1/I and 1/f². A rule "any clock/switching-current loop ≤ 1 cm²" therefore carries ≥ 10 dB margin under these assumptions and is a reasonable deterministic threshold — labelled as an estimate-derived heuristic, since the harmonic current is assumed rather than known.
- Below 30 MHz the radiated limits do not apply (conducted emissions do), and the fundamental signal frequencies of these boards (< 10 MHz) are not themselves the radiated risk; only harmonics of MCU clock/GPIO edges and relay switching transients are. [recall — CISPR 32 Class B radiated limits commonly cited as 30 dBµV/m at 10 m (≈ 40 dBµV/m at 3 m) for 30–230 MHz and 37 dBµV/m at 10 m for 230–1000 MHz; not verified this session, and the search tool's own guess of these numbers was inconsistent.]
- Out of scope, to be declared explicitly: radiated/conducted emissions pass/fail prediction, immunity (ESD/EFT/surge) performance, enclosure and cable resonances. Reasons: the earlier finding that Huygens-box prediction underestimates by > 10 dB at resonances; the inputs (harmonic currents, common-mode voltages, cable layout) are not in the Board IR; and the small-source formulas assume electrically small, non-resonant structures.

- CISPR 32 Class B limit table not verified from a primary or reliable secondary source.
- No published accuracy figure for the small-loop/short-cable formulas against measurement was retrieved.
- The LearnEMC 1.7 A example was not opened to resolve the discrepancy.
