## 1. Assurance cases: structure, defeaters, confidence, tooling, and auto-generation per board

### Takeaway
The existing "graded Claim + defeater register D1-D9" is already an informal eliminative-argumentation assurance case; the cheapest upgrade is to adopt Assurance 2.0's vocabulary (claims / argument steps / evidence, defeaters that are either *refuted* or *accepted as residual risk*, and a three-view confidence report: positive, negative, residual doubts) and emit it per board as machine-generated YAML rendered with gsn2x. Do not adopt full GSN/SACM tooling; adopt their node types and one rule: no claim is "supported" while an unresolved defeater points at it.

### Cited Findings
- **Assurance 2.0 manifesto**: Bloomfield & Rushby, "Assurance 2.0: A Manifesto", arXiv:2004.10474. Aim: make assurance "more rigorous, with increased focus on the reasoning and evidence employed, and explicit identification of defeaters and counterevidence." [S] — [arXiv 2004.10474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10474)
- **Defeaters in Assurance 2.0**: Bloomfield, Netkachova & Rushby, "Defeaters and Eliminative Argumentation in Assurance 2.0", SRI-CSL-2024-01 / arXiv:2405.15800 (May 2024). Defeaters "express doubts about aspects of the argument and can be developed into subcases that confirm or refute the doubts"; the report specifies how multiple levels of defeaters are represented and assessed in the Clarissa/ASCE tool. Eliminative argumentation is described as using "a negative argument to refute all reasons why the top claim could be false." [S] — [arXiv 2405.15800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15800), [SRI page](https://www.csl.sri.com/~rushby/abstracts/defeaters24)
- **Workflow for doubts**: a concern is first recorded as a *doubt* attached to the suspect node, then either dismissed or refined into a *defeater* with its own investigation; "exact defeaters introduce negation & refutation"; exploratory defeaters "must eventually be refuted or accepted as residual risks." [S] — [Assurance 2.0 in a Nutshell](https://www.csl.sri.com/~rushby/papers/a2nutshell.pdf), [arXiv 2405.15800](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15800v1)
- **Two kinds of defeat**: *undercutting* (the evidence is inadequate to justify the confidence required) vs *rebutting* (there is evidence that contradicts the claim). [S] — [arXiv 2405.15800](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15800v1)
- **Confidence**: Rushby & Bloomfield, "Assessing Confidence with Assurance 2.0", arXiv:2205.04522 (v4 May 2024): confidence "should be based on attributes that draw on three different perspectives: positive, negative, and residual doubts." [S] — [arXiv 2205.04522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04522)
- **Eliminative Argumentation (origin)**: Goodenough, Weinstock & Klein, "Eliminative Argumentation: A Basis for Arguing Confidence in System Properties", CMU/SEI-2015-TR-005, Feb 2015, DOI 10.1184/R1/6573413.v1 (free). Starts from the observation that there is no agreed way to explain what degree of confidence one should have in a case; components per secondary literature: Claims, Evidence, Inference Rules, Defeaters, Argument Terminators; relies on defeasible reasoning and recursive challenging of claims to progressively eliminate doubts. [S] — [SEI library](https://sei.cmu.edu/library/eliminative-argumentation-a-basis-for-arguing-confidence-in-system-properties), [secondary description in arXiv 2401.17991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.17991)
- **LLMs as defeater generators**: "Evaluating the Effectiveness of GPT-4 Turbo in Creating Defeaters for Assurance Cases", arXiv:2401.17991 — evidence that an LLM can be used to *propose* defeaters (a role compatible with "LLM proposes, deterministic tool disposes"). [S] — [arXiv 2401.17991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.17991)
- **GSN Community Standard v3** is maintained by the Safety-Critical Systems Club (SCSC) Assurance Case Working Group; the v2-to-v3 change document groups changes as: administrative, patterns (new "template" constructs for repeated fragments), modular notation, confidence argument, dialectic, other. [S] — [SCSC GSN v2 to v3 changes](https://www.scsc.uk/file/gc/GSNv2-to-v3_changes-1092.pdf)
- **OMG SACM**: a Claim is marked *defeated* when counter-argument/evidence proves it false; this is modelled by an `AssertedInference` flagged as a counter-argument. The same source states "There is no equivalence for defeated Claims in GSN" (written against GSN v2). SACM also supports argument packages with interfaces/citations for modular disclosure. [S] — [Wei et al., "Model Based System Assurance Using the Structured Assurance Case Metamodel", arXiv 1905.02427](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.02427)
- **Formal evidence inside an assurance case**: "Integration of Formal Proof into Unified Assurance Cases with Isabelle/SACM", arXiv:2009.12154 — precedent for attaching machine-checked proofs as evidence nodes. [S] — [arXiv 2009.12154](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12154)
- **gsn2x**: MIT-licensed tool by jonasthewolf that renders GSN from YAML to SVG; multiple input files are treated as modules. The repository front page does not state which checks it runs, the GSN version, or defeater support. [F] — [gsn2x GitHub](https://github.com/jonasthewolf/gsn2x)
- **Astah GSN** is a commercial (non-free, non-YAML) GSN editor. [S] — [ComponentSource: Astah GSN](https://www.componentsource.com/product/astah-gsn/about)

### Inferences
- **Mapping of the existing system**: D1-D9 are *undercutting* defeaters (they attack the adequacy of evidence, e.g. D2 "MCU modelled as a resistor" undercuts every simulation-derived claim). A bench measurement that contradicts a simulation is a *rebutting* defeater. Recording which kind each one is makes the per-board wording mechanical: undercut claims are worded "not shown", rebutted claims are worded "shown false".
- **Lightest viable representation (one developer)**: a JSON/YAML node list with five node kinds — `claim`, `strategy` (argument step: decomposition / substitution / evidence incorporation / concretion / calculation, the Assurance 2.0 block vocabulary — block names from memory, verify in arXiv 2004.10474), `evidence` (hash-bound artefact reference), `defeater` (state: open / refuted-by(evidence) / accepted-residual(justification, owner, expiry)), `assumption`. The top claim per board is the 7-branch decomposition (I-VII); each leaf is a check result carrying its trust rung.
- **Automatic emission**: `verify.py` findings already carry (rule id, rung, verdict). A generator walks a static *pattern* (the 7-branch tree with strategy nodes, authored once by hand and version-controlled) and instantiates leaves from the findings; any leaf with no finding becomes an explicit `undeveloped` goal (GSN's undeveloped-element concept; equivalent to "unchecked is unknown, never pass"). Render with gsn2x to SVG for the human; keep the JSON as the normative artefact. The pattern is the reviewed, trusted part; the instance is generated — this mirrors GSN v3 "patterns/templates".
- **Three-view confidence report per board** (direct from Assurance 2.0): (a) *positive* — which claims are supported and at what rung; (b) *negative* — which defeaters were raised and how each was refuted, with the evidence; (c) *residual doubts* — the defeaters accepted as residual, verbatim. This is more honest than a single score and needs no probability arithmetic.
- **Skip**: SACM XMI interchange, AdvoCATE, CertWare, Resolute/AADL, numeric confidence propagation (Dempster-Shafer / BBN). None changes a decision for a one-person project.
- **Use an LLM only to propose new defeaters** against the static pattern ("what would make this branch false?"); a human accepts them into the register. The LLM must never mark a defeater refuted.

### Gaps
- The GSN v3 text itself was not retrieved: exact semantics of "assurance claim points", confidence arguments and v3 dialectic (challenge/defeat relationships) are unverified here; check scsc.uk/gsn directly (free).
- Not verified this session (no source retrieved): AdvoCATE (NASA) capabilities and availability, CertWare, Resolute, "ACME"/"Socrates", Trustworthy Systems tools, CAE (Adelard) block definitions, "dynamic safety cases" (Denney, Pai & Habli 2015) — treat any claims about these as unsourced.
- Whether gsn2x validates references / reports unresolved elements / supports v3 dialectic nodes was not confirmed from the repo front page; read its docs before depending on it.
- SEI report's treatment of Baconian probability and its exact three-way defeater taxonomy (rebutting / undermining / undercutting) was not confirmed from the primary text.

## 2. IC sign-off as the template

### Takeaway
IC practice contributes three transferable mechanisms: (1) sign-off is done by a tool *different from* the implementation tool and often by *two* independent engines whose outputs are XOR-compared; (2) every rule in a deck has dedicated pass and fail test structures, with deck-level coverage accounting (rule in deck AND rule in tests) and a four-way outcome table; (3) the tape-out gate is a fixed, ordered list of checks that aborts on first failure. All three are implementable in Python/CI for a PCB engine.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenLane 2 Classic flow sign-off steps** (each individually gated by a config variable, most on by default): Magic stream-out and KLayout stream-out (`RUN_MAGIC_STREAMOUT`, `RUN_KLAYOUT_STREAMOUT`); **KLayout XOR of the two independently produced GDSII files** (`RUN_KLAYOUT_XOR`); Magic DRC (`RUN_MAGIC_DRC`); KLayout DRC (`RUN_KLAYOUT_DRC`); Magic SPICE extraction + Netgen LVS (`RUN_LVS`); multi-corner STA post-PnR via OpenROAD (`RUN_MCSTA`); parasitic extraction via OpenROAD.RCX (`RUN_SPEF_EXTRACTION`); IR-drop report (`RUN_IRDROP_REPORT`); formal equivalence via Yosys EQY (`RUN_EQY`, described as alpha-stage). [S] — [OpenLane 2 built-in flows reference](https://openlane2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/flows.html), [openlane on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/openlane)
- **Rule-deck QA with pass/fail structures**: test cases are not taken from real designs but drawn as simple shapes from the design manual; "fail test cases are designed to cause the runset to report an error, and the pass test cases are designed such that the runset should not report an error"; on mismatch either the runset or the design manual is corrected. [S] — [US 8,875,064 "Automated DRC test case generation"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8875064)
- **Outcome classification**: intentional fail, intentional pass, unintentional fail (pass-intended structure failed), unintentional pass (fail-intended structure passed), missing boundary. [S] — [US 6,063,132 "Method for verifying design rule checking software"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6063132)
- **Per-rule error layer + filter layer** so that each QA cell is judged only against its own rule; first run is manually verified and stored as "expected" results, thereafter auto-compared whenever the runset or tool version changes. [S] — [US 6,606,735 (Synopsys)](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6606735)
- **Runset test coverage**: QA should ideally be 100% complete and "execute every line of the runset"; tooling reports uncovered rules and proposes test cases. [S] — [US 7,254,791](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7254791)
- **Open worked example — IHP Open-PDK KLayout DRC regression**: per-rule unit tests grouped by rule table; golden results in `unit_golden` generated by `gen_golden.py` "for all implemented and verified DRC rules"; `run_regression.py` writes `all_test_cases_results.csv` and `rule_deck_rules.csv`; a rule passes iff `viol_not_golden = 0` (no false positives) and `golden_not_viol = 0` (no false negatives); coverage flags `in_tests = 1` and `in_rule_deck = 1`. [F] — [IHP Open-PDK DRC Testing](https://ihp-open-pdk-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/verification/drc/04_05_testing.html)
- **GlobalFoundries open PDK** regression script organises test GDS into per-rule pass and fail folders and reports pass patterns, fail patterns, false negatives, false positives, and *not-tested rules*. [S, partial snippet] — [run_regression_drc.py](https://foss-eda-tools.googlesource.com/globalfoundries-new-nda/+/HEAD/scripts/run_regression_drc.py)
- **Shuttle precheck gate (Efabless mpw_precheck / ChipFoundry cf-precheck)**: "a sequence of checks that aborts with the appropriate error message(s) if any of them fails"; includes XOR of the submitted wrapper against the golden wrapper outside the user area, GPIO-defines check, KLayout DRC (FEOL, off-grid, density), optional Magic DRC, LVS, licence/documentation checks (skippable in private mode), manifest check against the current golden harness. Users report that the gate's KLayout ruleset can differ from the local one, producing new errors at the gate. [S; sources are mirrors/older snapshots] — [mpw_precheck README (shuttle snapshot)](https://foss-eda-tools.googlesource.com/third_party/shuttle/sky130/mpw-003/slot-030/+show/82987145c167488d84fae9f226487a9189367251/mpw_precheck/README.md), [cf-precheck](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fchipfoundry%2Fcf-precheck)
- **Qualification-by-agreement precedent**: NVIDIA's DRC-Coder judges LLM-generated DRC code by F1 against a commercial DRC tool (F1 = 1.000 vs 0.631 for plain prompting, sub-3nm node) — i.e. an in-house checker is qualified by precision/recall against a reference engine. [S] — [NVIDIA Research: DRC-Coder](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2024-11_drc-coder-automated-drc-checker-code-generation-using-llm-autonomous-agent)

### Inferences
- **IC → PCB mapping (what transfers)**:

| IC sign-off | PCB analogue in Circuit OS | 7-branch |
|---|---|---|
| DRC (two engines + XOR) | in-house DRC kernel + KiCad `kicad-cli pcb drc` on exported board; compare violation sets | I |
| Stream-out XOR (Magic GDS vs KLayout GDS) | Gerber/drill emitted by own writer vs Gerber plotted by KiCad from the exported `.kicad_pcb`; polygon XOR per layer | I, III |
| LVS (extract netlist from layout, compare to schematic) | extract connectivity from *emitted Gerbers/IPC-D-356*, not from Board IR; compare with circuit-IR netlist (union-find judge) | III |
| ERC | schematic ERC + pin-type rules | VI |
| PEX/RCX + STA / IR-drop / EM | trace R/L/C extraction, current-density and voltage-drop bounds, SPICE re-simulation with parasitics | V |
| Antenna | no direct analogue (process-charging effect); skip | — |
| LEC | translation validation between IR levels (see section 3) | III, VI |
| DFM / litho hotspots | fab capability rules (annular ring, acid traps, solder-mask slivers), assembly rules | I, II |
| Corners / guardbands | tolerance boxes in z3 proofs; fab tolerance corners (etch, drill wander, registration) | V, VI |
| mpw_precheck | `gate()` as ordered abort-on-fail list with logged tool/rule-deck versions | all |

- **What does not transfer**: there is no foundry-supplied golden rule deck for PCB (fab "capabilities" pages are prose, and differ per fab and per price tier), no standard-cell characterisation equivalent for discrete parts (datasheets are the only "library", hence D7), and no mandatory LVS culture — which is exactly why branches III and IV need explicit construction.
- **Deck-QA rule for the DRC kernel**: for each rule R with threshold t, a generated coupon with a `pass` instance at t and t+δ and a `fail` instance at t-δ (δ = one grid unit), plus the CSV contract from IHP: `in_tests`, `in_rule_deck`, `viol_not_golden`, `golden_not_viol`. CI fails if any rule has `in_tests = 0` (that is the "not-tested rules" column). The "unintentional pass" cell is the dangerous one and should block release; "unintentional fail" should block too but can be waived.
- **The mpw_precheck lesson about version skew**: the gate must pin and record the rule-deck hash it ran; "passes locally, fails at gate" is the symptom of unpinned decks.
- "Correct-by-construction" (generators that cannot emit a violation) reduces defect *rate* but is not evidence; the IC flow still runs verify-after sign-off on correct-by-construction output. Keep both and never let a constructive guarantee substitute for the check.

### Gaps
- No primary Siemens/Synopsys/Cadence white paper on "golden sign-off tool vs implementation tool" was retrieved; the independence-of-sign-off principle is supported here by OpenLane's dual-engine + XOR design and patents, not by a vendor statement.
- No foundry-published deck-qualification *procedure* (only patents from ~1999-2008 and two open PDKs) — acceptance criteria used by TSMC/GF internally are not public.
- OpenLane 2's CVC and antenna-check status in the current Classic flow was not confirmed.
- Corner/guardband methodology (OCV, AOCV, sign-off margins) was not researched; nothing sourced.

## 3. Translation validation, certifying algorithms, and a minimal trusted base

### Takeaway
Do not try to verify the router/placer/footprint generator; make each one emit a *witness* and validate every run with a checker that is far simpler than the algorithm (certifying algorithms) — this is per-run translation validation, and CompCert itself uses it for register allocation rather than proving the allocator. The trusted computing base then shrinks to the small checkers plus the format parsers.

### Cited Findings
- **Translation validation**: Pnueli, Siegel & Singerman, "Translation Validation", TACAS 1998, LNCS 1384, pp. 151-166, DOI 10.1007/BFb0054170. Rather than proving the compiler correct in advance, "each run of the compiler is followed by a validation phase" that checks the produced target against the source; motivation: compiler verification is extremely complex and every change to the compiler requires redoing the proof. [S] — [doi:10.1007/BFb0054170](https://doi.org/10.1007%2FBFb0054170)
- **Necula**, "Translation Validation for an Optimizing Compiler", PLDI 2000, pp. 83-95: validates GCC by comparing the intermediate form before and after each pass; unlike Pnueli et al. it needs no help from the optimiser, using heuristics to recover what the transformation did. [S] — [paper PDF](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~sbansal/csl862-soft/readings/translation_validation.pdf)
- **CompCert uses verified validators**: register allocation is implemented as untrusted OCaml graph-colouring heuristics and "checked by the validator method"; Rideau & Leroy, "Validating Register Allocation and Spilling" (CC 2010) give an a-posteriori validation algorithm based on backward dataflow inference whose soundness is proved in Coq. [S] — [Rideau & Leroy PDF](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~rideau/docs/papers/RideauLeroy.pdf), [Leroy, "Formal verification of a realistic compiler", CACM 2009](https://xavierleroy.org/publi/compcert-CACM.pdf)
- **Certifying algorithms**: McConnell, Mehlhorn, Näher & Schweitzer, "Certifying algorithms", Computer Science Review 5(2):119-161, May 2011. A certifying algorithm "produces, with each output, a certificate or witness (easy-to-verify proof) that the particular output has not been compromised by a bug"; the authors argue that for complex algorithmic tasks only certifying algorithms are satisfactory, and prove the concept is universal; it is the design principle of the LEDA library. Course slides distinguish strongly certifying / certifying / weakly certifying by how illegal inputs are handled. [S] — [ANU record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/certifying-algorithms/), [Mehlhorn talk abstract](https://www.cs.umd.edu/node/16134), [slides](https://users-cs.au.dk/~gerth/ae15/slides/certifying.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Witness designs per generator (all checkable in O(n) to O(n log n) with no search)**:
  - *Router*: witness = for each net, the ordered list of segments/vias forming a spanning tree over its pads. Checker: (a) every segment endpoint coincides with a pad or another segment endpoint of the same net on a shared layer (connectivity by union-find — the existing independent judge is already this); (b) pairwise clearance between different-net copper via a sweep line or uniform grid hash. The A* search, cost function and rip-up logic are out of the TCB.
  - *"Unroutable" claims*: the router must not be trusted to say "impossible". A negative result needs its own certificate (a cut whose capacity < demand, or a pigeonhole on a channel) or else is rung `unknown`. The prior "phantom-unrouted" bug is the canonical reason.
  - *Lower bounds/floors*: half-perimeter wirelength (HPWL) or rectilinear MST as a certificate that reported length ≥ floor; a route shorter than the floor proves a checker or router bug.
  - *Footprint generator*: witness = the datasheet dimension table row (with page citation + record hash) and the derivation (IPC-7351-style arithmetic). Checker recomputes pad geometry from the dimension record and compares to the emitted footprint; it does not share the generator's code path, only the record.
  - *Placement*: witness = courtyard polygons; checker = pairwise non-overlap + inside board outline + keep-outs.
  - *Netlist → Board IR → Gerber*: translation validation at each IR boundary — re-extract the netlist from the *lower* representation with an independent parser and compare to the upper one (this is LVS).
- **TCB statement to publish** (de Bruijn-criterion spirit: small independent proof checker instead of trusting the prover): list exactly what must be right for a `pass` to be true — the witness checkers (target: a few hundred lines each, no shared imports with generators), the Gerber/drill/IPC-D-356 parsers, the rule deck values, z3/sympy, Python. Everything else (LLM, generators, router, optimiser) is explicitly untrusted. Measure TCB in lines of code and track it in CI; growth is a review trigger.
- **The shared-IR gap is a TCB problem**: when verifier and generator both read Board IR and the pin-map table, those two artefacts are *in* the TCB without being checked. Remedy = make the checker consume the *emitted fabrication artefacts* (Gerber X2 / drill / IPC-D-356 / pick-and-place) on one side and the *schematic-level netlist + datasheet records* on the other, never the Board IR.

### Gaps
- The de Bruijn criterion and "proof-carrying code" (Necula 1997) were not sourced this session; cite Barendregt & Wiedijk "The challenge of computer mathematics" (2005) and Necula POPL 1997 after verification.
- No published work was found applying certifying algorithms or translation validation specifically to PCB routers/footprint generators; the mapping above is inference.

## 4. Independence and common-mode failure

### Takeaway
Diversity helps but independence cannot be assumed: Knight & Leveson showed that independently written versions fail together far more often than independence predicts, because people err on the same hard parts of the *specification*. For Circuit OS the specification-level common mode is the shared Board IR and pin-map table, so the fix is data diversity (check from emitted artefacts and from a separately sourced pin map), a third-party engine as a second channel, and — the cheapest move in every tool-qualification standard — arranging that each tool's output is checked by something else so that the tool itself needs no qualification.

### Cited Findings
- **Knight & Leveson**, "An Experimental Evaluation of the Assumption of Independence in Multi-Version Programming", IEEE TSE 12(1):96-109, 1986: 27 versions written independently from one specification at two universities, each passing a 200-input acceptance test, then run on one million tests; the number of tests on which more than one version failed was substantially higher than independence predicts; a course summary of the data gives 1,256 multiple-failure cases vs 21,257 single-failure cases and rejection of independence at 99% confidence. Explanation: programmers "make mistakes on the same hard parts of the problem." [S] — [paper PDF](https://www.kth.se/social/files/564df871f2765419e306178d/KnightLeveson.pdf), [Chalmers lecture summary](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2012/course/EDA122/lectures-2012/lecture12-2012.pdf)
- The authors' own narrower statement: the independence assumption "did not hold for our programs and, therefore, … reliability improvement predictions using models based on this assumption may be unrealistically optimistic." [S] — [Knight & Leveson reply to critics](https://www.cs.washington.edu/homes/leveson/critics.pdf)
- **ISO 26262-8 tool confidence** (paywalled; table reproduced consistently in secondary sources): TI1 = no possibility that a tool malfunction introduces or fails to detect an error, TI2 = otherwise; TD1/TD2/TD3 = high/medium/low-or-unknown confidence that a malfunction is prevented or detected. TCL = TCL1 for any TI1; for TI2: TD1→TCL1, TD2→TCL2, TD3→TCL3. TCL1 needs no qualification. Determination is **per use case, not per tool**. [S] — [Synopsys white paper](https://www.synopsys.com/content/dam/synopsys/solutions/documents/automotive-tool-confidence-iso-26262-wp.pdf), [Hillebrand et al., SAFECOMP 2012](https://wwwbroy.in.tum.de/~philipps/pub/safecomp2012.pdf), [ADI EngineerZone](https://ez.analog.com/ez-blogs/b/engineerzone-spotlight/posts/iso-26262-essentials-ensuring-confidence-in-your-software-tools)
- **DO-330 / DO-178C §12.2** (paywalled; from vendor briefings): Criteria 1 = tool whose output is part of the product and could insert an error; Criteria 2 = verification tool that could fail to detect an error *and* whose output is used to justify eliminating/reducing other verification or development processes; Criteria 3 = verification tool that could fail to detect an error. TQL by software level A/B/C/D: Criteria 1 → TQL-1/2/3/4; Criteria 2 → TQL-4/4/5/5; Criteria 3 → TQL-5 always. Qualification is not required when the tool's output is itself verified per DO-178C §6. [S] — [LDRA DO-330 briefing](https://ldra.com/wp-content/uploads/ldra/DO-330-Test-Tool-Qualification-Technical-Briefing-v2.0.pdf), [AdaCore DO-330 document](https://www.adacore.com/uploads_gems/do-330-ed-215-tool-qualification-document.pdf), [Pothon, "Dissimilar Tools"](https://www.adacore.com/uploads/books/pdf/Dissimilar_Tools_V1.pdf)
- **DO-178C independence**: "with independence" means the verifier of an item is not its author, documented; Level A has 71 objectives of which 30 with independence (most sources; one source says 33 — conflict noted). [S] — [Wikipedia: DO-178C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-178C), [Rapita DO-178C guidance](https://www.rapitasystems.com/node/528)
- **IEC 61508 on diverse software** (paywalled; secondary): software has only systematic failures, so identical channels leave software as a single point of failure; IEC 61508-2 limits the systematic-capability gain from combining two items to one level, plausibly because hidden common causes (e.g. shared tools) may remain; IEC 61508-3 Annex A Table A.2 lists diverse-architecture options and Table A.10 calls for software common-cause failure analysis. [S, single blog source] — [ADI EngineerZone: Is implementing diverse software worth the effort?](https://ez.analog.com/ez-blogs/b/engineerzone-spotlight/posts/is-implementing-diverse-software-worth-the-effort)
- **Differential testing**: McKeeman, "Differential Testing for Software", Digital Technical Journal 10(1):100-107, 1998 — generate random valid programs, feed them to two or more compilers (or one compiler with different switches), flag disagreement, then reduce the failing input. [S] — [DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/40/4856), [related patent US 5,754,860](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5754860)
- **EDA-tool fuzzing (current)**: Cosgrove, Donaldson & Wickerson, "Finding and understanding bugs in FPGA place-and-route engines", FPGA '26 (tool FUZNET; equivalence checking as oracle; "no prior work scrutinises the correctness of P&R engines directly"); SynFuzz (arXiv:2504.18812; netlist-level differential fuzzing, 7 new synthesis bugs); VERMEI (arXiv:2508.15536; mutation + differential testing of Yosys/Vivado/Quartus, 15 bugs reported, 9 confirmed new); HeisenTrojans (arXiv:2312.13190; EDA tools are "prone to software bugs"). [S] — [FUZNET paper](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~afd/papers/2026/FPGA.pdf), [SynFuzz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18812), [VERMEI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15536v1), [HeisenTrojans](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2312.13190)
- **KiCad as a scriptable diverse channel**: `kicad-cli pcb drc` supports `--exit-code-violations` (exit 0 = none, 5 = violations), `--severity-*` filters including `--severity-exclusions`, and JSON output in which excluded items carry `"excluded": true`. Known caveats: forum report of CLI results differing from GUI; GitLab issue #24264 reports custom-rule (`.kicad_dru`) exceptions surfacing as errors from the CLI. [S] — [KiCad 9 CLI reference](https://docs.kicad.org/9.0/en/cli/cli.html), [KiCad forum thread](https://forum.kicad.info/t/cli-erc-drc-gives-different-output-than-running-manually-in-gui/55207), [KiCad issue 24264](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/issues/24264)

### Inferences
- **Classify every Circuit OS component by the ISO 26262 / DO-330 logic** (use the *method*, ignore the paperwork):
  - Generators/router = TI2 (can introduce an error) / Criteria 1. If and only if an independent check covers their output, TD1 → TCL1 → no qualification needed. This is the formal justification for "untrusted generator + small checker".
  - `verify.py`, DRC kernel = TI2 (can fail to detect) / Criteria 2 the moment a `pass` is used to skip a bench test or a human review; Criteria 3 otherwise. So the checkers are what must be "qualified" — by section 5's test suite — and their qualification is void for any use case not covered by tests ("per use case, not per tool").
  - The qualification artefact for a one-person project = (i) stated intended use, (ii) the known-bad zoo results, (iii) mutation score, (iv) agreement statistics with KiCad DRC, all pinned to the checker's content hash.
- **Measuring independence when data models are shared** — a dependency audit, done by machine:
  1. *Import graph*: checker modules may not import generator modules (enforce with an import-linter rule in CI).
  2. *Input provenance*: for each check, record whether its inputs are (a) emitted artefacts re-parsed, (b) Board IR, (c) shared tables. Rung is capped at `evidence`/`bounded` for (b)/(c) unless the shared item is itself checked elsewhere.
  3. *Common-cause register*: list each shared assumption (pin-map table, unit convention, layer naming, coordinate origin/Y-axis sign, footprint rotation convention, net-naming) and for each name the diverse channel that would catch it — or record it as an open defeater.
  4. *Empirical coincidence*: on the seeded-defect corpus, count defects missed by both channels vs by each alone; a both-missed rate above the product of the single-miss rates is measured positive correlation (the Knight-Leveson statistic). With small n, report counts, not rates.
- **Cheapest diverse channels available** (ranked by independence gained per hour): (1) export `.kicad_pcb` and run `kicad-cli pcb drc --format json --severity-all --exit-code-violations` — different authors, different geometry kernel, different data model; (2) re-extract netlist from emitted Gerber/IPC-D-356 with a separate parser and compare against schematic netlist; (3) pin map: second source = the manufacturer's symbol/footprint from a different library (e.g. CERN library, vendor CAD) compared pin-by-pin with the in-house table — disagreement is a hard stop, agreement is still only "two libraries agree" (they may copy the same datasheet typo); (4) the fab's own DFM report and flying-probe result as an outsourced third channel.
- **Do not build a second in-house checker from the same spec** — Knight & Leveson is the argument that it buys little. Prefer *functional* diversity (different input artefact, different principle) over *implementation* diversity.
- Pin KiCad version in CI and treat CLI/GUI discrepancies and custom-rule handling as a known tool defect to work around (use JSON output and do the filtering yourself rather than relying on exit code + KiCad-side exclusions).

### Gaps
- IEC 61508-3 table contents and IEC 61508-7 technique descriptions ("diverse monitor", "software diversity") were not verified against the standard — one blog source only.
- DO-178C "30 vs 33 objectives with independence" conflict unresolved; check RTCA Annex A.
- No source found that quantifies independence between a generator and checker sharing a data model; the audit above is inference. Littlewood & Miller's and Eckhardt & Lee's models of coincident failure were not retrieved.
- "Cir-Fuzzer" was not found under that name; the EDA fuzzing papers above are the verified substitutes.

## 5. Testing the checkers themselves

### Takeaway
A checker is qualified by showing it (a) fires on every defect class it claims to detect and (b) stays silent on clean inputs, both at the boundary — i.e. the foundry deck-QA discipline plus mutation analysis of the checker code, metamorphic relations where no oracle exists, and a permanent "known-bad zoo" with labelled defects from which precision and recall are computed per rule.

### Cited Findings
- **Oracle problem**: Barr, Harman, McMinn, Shahbaz & Yoo, "The Oracle Problem in Software Testing: A Survey", IEEE TSE 41(5):507-525, 2015, DOI 10.1109/TSE.2014.2372785 — "Given an input for a system, the challenge of distinguishing the corresponding desired, correct behaviour from potentially incorrect behavior." [S] — [UCL Discovery (open access)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1471263/)
- **Metamorphic testing surveys**: Segura, Fraser, Sanchez & Ruiz-Cortés, "A Survey on Metamorphic Testing", IEEE TSE 42(9):805-824, 2016, DOI 10.1109/TSE.2016.2532875; Chen, Kuo, Liu, Poon, Towey, Tse & Zhou, "Metamorphic Testing: A Review of Challenges and Opportunities", ACM Computing Surveys 51(1), Article 4, 2018, DOI 10.1145/3143561 — MT addresses both test-case generation and result verification by relating outputs of transformed inputs. [S] — [Segura et al. PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110335/1/segura16-tse.pdf), [Chen et al.](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/51607)
- **Mutation testing in Python**: Cosmic Ray "operates by making small changes to a program's source code and then running a test suite; if the test suite ever succeeds on mutated code then a flag is raised." A comparison study of Python mutation tools reported large differences in the fraction of "competent" mutants across tools (Poodle highest, Cosmic Ray next, mutmut inconclusive due to incomplete results). [S] — [EuroPython 2024: Cosmic Ray](https://ep2024.europython.eu/session/mutation-testing-in-python-with-cosmic-ray), [tool comparison](https://emerging-researchers.org/projects/413-3/), [SBQS comparison paper](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/sbqs/article/view/32948)
- **Property-based testing**: Hypothesis — "you write tests which should pass for all inputs in whatever range you describe, and let Hypothesis randomly choose which of those inputs to check"; on failure it reports a minimised example. [S] — [Hypothesis docs](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/hypothesis/)
- **Rule-deck coverage criteria** (pass + fail structure per rule; per-rule false-positive/false-negative counters; "rule in tests" and "rule in deck" flags; not-tested-rules report): see section 2. [F/S] — [IHP DRC testing](https://ihp-open-pdk-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/verification/drc/04_05_testing.html), [GF regression script](https://foss-eda-tools.googlesource.com/globalfoundries-new-nda/+/HEAD/scripts/run_regression_drc.py)
- **Benchmarks/corpora with real boards**: PCBWorld (arXiv:2607.05915) is an interactive environment built on the KiCad engine with "679 real open-source boards" plus controllable synthetic instances and eight engine-checked metrics applicable to any completed board regardless of routing method; OmniRouting (Aug 2026) has 1,681 industrial schematic-coupled designs; PCB-Bench (ICLR 2026) has ~3,700 expert-annotated text instances and 170+ complete projects. [S] — [PCBWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05915), [OmniRouting](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04434), [PCB-Bench](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009621)
- **CERN KiCad component library** (May 2026): 17,000+ symbols/footprints released as open source by CERN's Design Office; one report says CERN-OHL-P (permissive) — licence variant not confirmed from the primary page. CERN-OHL v2 has three variants: -P (permissive), -W (weakly reciprocal), -S (strongly reciprocal). [S] — [CERN open science announcement](https://openscience.web.cern.ch/node/477), [Help Net Security report](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/?p=370677), [OHWR licence discussion](https://ohwr.org/project/diot-crate-hw/issues/10)
- **Checker-vs-reference accounting**: precision/recall (F1) against a reference DRC engine is the published acceptance metric for a generated checker (DRC-Coder). [S] — [NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2024-11_drc-coder-automated-drc-checker-code-generation-using-llm-autonomous-agent)

### Inferences
- **Checker qualification suite — minimum set (all automatable, all in pytest)**:
  1. *Boundary pair per rule* (deck QA): generated coupon with instances at threshold-δ (must fire), threshold (must not), threshold+δ (must not). Generate these from the rule table itself so adding a rule without a coupon is impossible (CI: `in_tests == in_rule_deck`).
  2. *Sentinel/canary*: in every production run, inject one known violation per rule family into a scratch copy of the board and assert the checker reports it. A checker that silently short-circuits (empty input, unit bug, exception swallowed) fails the canary. This converts "0 violations" from ambiguous to meaningful and is the direct enforcement of "unchecked is unknown, never pass".
  3. *Mutation analysis of checker code* (cosmic-ray or mutmut restricted to the checker modules — the TCB): surviving mutants in comparison operators and thresholds are exactly the missing boundary tests. Report mutation score per checker; triage survivors into equivalent / test-gap. Run nightly, not per commit.
  4. *Artefact mutation (seeded defects)*: mutate *boards*, not code — delete a segment, shift a pad by one grid unit, swap two pins in the pin map, mirror a footprint, rotate 90/180, swap layers, change drill size, short two nets with a sliver. Each operator carries the label of the rule(s) that must fire → labelled set → per-rule recall. Clean boards from the golden corpus give false-positive counts → precision.
  5. *Metamorphic relations for geometry* (no oracle needed): verdict invariant under translation, rotation by 90°, mirroring (with layer swap), unit change mm↔mil, net renaming, component reordering, uniform scaling of geometry *and* rules; monotonicity — tightening a clearance rule never removes a violation; adding copper never removes a clearance violation; removing a segment never adds connectivity. (The existing 40/40 result fits here; extend with monotonicity relations, which catch threshold-direction bugs that invariances miss.)
  6. *Property-based generators* (Hypothesis): strategies that build small random boards by construction-with-known-truth (place k pads, connect by a random spanning tree → connectivity ground truth is known by construction; then perturb). Use integer nanometre coordinates to avoid float flakiness; let Hypothesis shrink to minimal counterexamples and commit each shrunk counterexample to the zoo.
  7. *Differential run* against KiCad DRC on the same boards; triage every disagreement into {our FN, our FP, KiCad FN, KiCad FP, rule-definition difference}. The last class is a list of documented semantic differences, itself a deliverable.
- **Known-bad board zoo — layout**: `zoo/<defect-class>/<id>/{board.*, label.yaml}` where `label.yaml` = {expected rule ids, branch I-VII, origin: seeded | shrunk-counterexample | field-escape, checker version that first caught it}. Rule: every bug ever found in router/DRC/verifier and every bench surprise adds an entry *before* the fix is merged. Zoo is append-only.
- **Defect-population estimate (capture-recapture)**: if channel A (in-house) finds a defects, channel B (KiCad DRC or a review) finds b, and c are common, the Lincoln-Petersen estimate of total defects is a·b/c. Only meaningful if channels are roughly independent (section 4) and c > 0; with the small counts realistic here, report it as an order-of-magnitude sanity check, not a claim.
- **Seeded-defect caveat**: recall on seeded defects is an upper-ish bound on recall for real defects, because seeds are drawn from the defect classes you already thought of. Record this as a standing defeater on every recall figure.
- **Golden corpus sourcing**: prefer boards that have demonstrably been manufactured (PCBWorld's 679 open-source KiCad boards, KiCad's bundled demos, OSHWA-certified projects, Antmicro/SparkFun/Adafruit repos). Since Circuit OS is AGPL-3.0, keep third-party boards *out of the repository*: store a manifest of (URL, commit hash, licence SPDX id, content hash) and fetch in CI. That avoids CC BY-SA / CERN-OHL-S / GPL compatibility questions entirely.
- **What a "clean real board" proves**: only false-positive behaviour (and even then real boards contain waived violations). Recall can only come from labelled defects.

### Gaps
- Licences of specific corpora were **not verified**: KiCad demo projects, SparkFun (commonly stated CC BY-SA 4.0), Adafruit (commonly CC BY-SA), Antmicro (commonly Apache-2.0), OSHWA directory terms, PCBWorld dataset licence. Check each repo's LICENSE file before any redistribution.
- ISPD routing benchmarks are IC-oriented; no verification that any is usable for PCB. No public "known-bad PCB" labelled defect corpus was found.
- mutmut's and cosmic-ray's handling of equivalent mutants was not found in sources; no cited typical mutation-score thresholds.
- Capture-recapture for software defects (e.g. Eick et al. 1992; Briand et al. 2000) was not retrieved; the formula above is textbook but uncited here.
- No literature found on metamorphic relations specifically for PCB DRC or routers.

## 6. Soundness vocabulary and combining evidence into an honest per-board verdict

### Takeaway
Never multiply or average rungs: report the chain's weakest rung per branch (NASA-STD-7009 uses exactly this lowest-score rule for input pedigree), keep "analysis" and "test" evidence as separate lines, and attach statistical statements only where trials are genuinely repeated and the population is stated — with zero failures in n trials the defensible claim is "failure rate below about 3/n at 95% confidence", which for n in the tens is a weak claim and should be worded as such.

### Cited Findings
- **Rule of three**: Hanley & Lippman-Hand, "If Nothing Goes Wrong, Is Everything All Right? Interpreting Zero Numerators", JAMA 249(13):1743-1745, 1983 — with 0 events in n trials the 95% upper confidence limit on the event rate is approximately 3/n; examples in the literature: 0/50 → ≤ 6%, 0/120 → ≤ 2.5%, 0/1000 → ≤ 0.3%. Recommended only for n > ~30; says nothing about the rate being zero. [S] — [JAMA abstract](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/389714), [BMJ ADC statistical reminder](https://adc.bmj.com/content/archdischild/90/6/642.2.full.pdf)
- **Conservative Bayesian claims / probability of perfection**: Zhao, Littlewood, Povyakalo & Wright, "Conservative Claims about the Probability of Perfection of Software-based Systems", ISSRE 2015; Zhao, Littlewood, Povyakalo, Strigini & Wright, Reliability Engineering & System Safety 175:265-282, 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.ress.2018.03.032. For software "sufficiently simple to be possibly perfect", model a prior probability of perfection and update on failure-free operation; since a full prior over pfd cannot be justified, derive results *guaranteed conservative* under partial priors. Earlier result (Littlewood & Rushby 2012; Littlewood & Povyakalo 2013): for a 1-out-of-2 system there is a simple conservative bound on system pfd in terms of the pfd of one channel and the probability of *non-perfection* (pnp) of the other. [S] — [City Research Online: ISSRE 2015](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12803/), [City Research Online: RESS 2018](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/19341/)
- **NASA-STD-7009A** (2016; free): eight credibility factors — data pedigree, input pedigree, code verification, solution verification, conceptual validation, referent validation, results uncertainty, results robustness — each scored on an ordinal 0-4 scale (0 = insufficient evidence). The scale does not measure credibility directly; it rates the rigour of the processes. Baseline guidance: "the lowest input data quality must be used to represent the entire set of input data" (a NASA presentation notes this can be overly pessimistic). Reports to decision-makers must include an uncertainty estimate and its basis. The 2008 baseline used a different eight (verification, validation, input pedigree, results uncertainty, results robustness, use history, M&S management, people qualifications). [S] — [NTRS 20230000585 (application of 7009A factors)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230000585), [NTRS 20090005963 (baseline CAS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20090005963/downloads/20090005963.pdf), [NTRS 20160011121](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160011121/downloads/20160011121.pdf)
- A vendor glossary states NASA-STD-7009B was approved 5 March 2024 as successor — unverified against NASA. [S] — [Validas glossary](https://www.validas.de/resources/glossary/nasa-std-7009b)
- **Assurance 2.0** reports confidence from positive, negative and residual-doubt perspectives rather than a single number. [S] — [arXiv 2205.04522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04522)

### Inferences
- **Vocabulary pinned to the existing rungs**:
  - `exact` = sound and complete for the stated property over the stated model (e.g. integer-geometry connectivity).
  - `sound over-approximation` = may report false violations, never misses one, *relative to the model* (abstract-interpretation sense). A `pass` here is strong; a `fail` may be spurious.
  - `bounded` = exhaustive only within a stated bound (tolerance box, corner set, depth, mesh) — bounded-model-checking sense; a `pass` is "no counterexample within bound B", and B must be printed.
  - `evidence` = testing/simulation/measurement on samples; supports but does not entail.
  - `unknown` = not checked or checker abstained.
  - Add an orthogonal field the rungs currently lack: **model validity** — every rung is "sound w.r.t. a model"; D2 (MCU as resistor) and D7 (datasheet figures unverified) are defeaters on the *model*, not on the checker. An `exact` verdict over an unvalidated model is honestly `exact-on-model / model unvalidated`.
- **Composition rules (deterministic, no arithmetic on confidence)**:
  1. *Serial chain* (claim depends on A and B): rung = min(rung A, rung B) in the order exact > sound-OA > bounded > evidence > unknown. Same as the 7009 lowest-pedigree rule.
  2. *Conjunction across branches I-VII*: report a vector of seven (verdict, rung, open defeaters); the scalar summary is the minimum, but always print the vector.
  3. *Redundant diverse channels on the same claim* do **not** raise the rung label (two `evidence` do not make a `bounded`); they are recorded as an agreement note and they *retire the specific common-cause defeater* they address.
  4. *Any decidable `fail` dominates* (gate refuses) regardless of rung elsewhere; an `unknown` never dominates to pass.
- **Statistics a one-developer project can actually claim** (exact zero-failure bound: p_upper = 1 - α^(1/n); my derivation):
  - n = 5 failure-free boards → 95% upper bound on per-board failure probability ≈ 45% (1 - 0.05^(1/5)); 50% confidence ≈ 13%.
  - n = 10 → ≈ 26% at 95%. n = 29 → ≈ 10%. n = 59 → ≈ 5%. n = 299 → ≈ 1%.
  - Therefore a 5-board fab run says almost nothing about *board-level* failure rate. The leverage is to change the unit of trial: 5 boards × 200 solder joints, or one coupon with 60 instances of a footprint, or 300 daisy-chained vias gives n in the hundreds *for that feature class*. Design the coupon so each feature class reaches n ≥ 59 (5% at 95%) or n ≥ 299 (1%).
  - Trials must be exchangeable with production use: 60 instances of a 0603 footprint say nothing about a QFN footprint. State the population in the claim.
  - Systematic (design) errors are not sampled by repetition: 300 copies of a wrong footprint fail 300 times. Repetition estimates *process* variation; *design* correctness needs one instance of each distinct design object — hence "every footprint at least once" (coverage), not "many of one" (sampling). Keep these two notions separate in the report.
- **"Possibly perfect" argument for the small checkers**: the Littlewood-Rushby 1-out-of-2 result is the formal reason a tiny checker is valuable — system failure probability is conservatively bounded by pfd(generator channel) × pnp(checker). A checker small enough to review exhaustively plus a high mutation score is the informal basis for a low pnp. Do not put a number on pnp; cite the structure of the argument only.
- **Honest verdict wording template** (generated, never hand-written):
  - `REFUSED: <n> decidable failures (list).`
  - or `EMITTED. Proven on the model: <claims at exact/sound>. Shown within bounds: <claims + bounds>. Supported by tests only: <claims + n trials + population>. Not checked: <list>. Model assumptions not validated against hardware: <defeater ids>. This board has not been built or measured.`
  - Ban the words "verified", "correct", "works" without a rung qualifier; lint for them in generated text.

### Gaps
- Littlewood & Rushby 2012 (IEEE TSE "Reasoning about the reliability of diverse two-channel systems in which one channel is 'possibly perfect'") was not retrieved directly — details via the 2015/2018 follow-ups only. Bishop & Bloomfield's conservative worst-case bound and Littlewood & Strigini 1993 ("Validation of ultra-high dependability") were not retrieved.
- How IEC 61508 / EN 50128 formally combine analysis and test evidence (technique tables by SIL) was not sourced.
- Abstract interpretation (Cousot & Cousot 1977) and BMC (Biere et al. 1999) primary references not retrieved; vocabulary definitions above are standard usage, uncited.

## 7. V&V frameworks and what each physical test detects

### Takeaway
ASME V&V 40's risk-informed logic (question of interest → context of use → model risk = model influence × decision consequence → credibility goals) is the right-sized framework for deciding how much validation each simulation-derived claim needs; NASA-STD-7009's eight factors give the checklist; and PCOLA/SOQ/FAM gives a ready-made coverage matrix for stating what each physical test does and does not see. Bare-board electrical test covers only shorts/opens against a reference that may itself be derived from the Gerbers — so it is not evidence for branch III unless an independent netlist is supplied.

### Cited Findings
- **ASME V&V 40-2018** (paywalled; framework summarised in open literature and adopted in FDA guidance): credibility = trust, established through evidence, in a model's predictive capability *for a context of use*; context of use = the role and scope of the model in answering the question of interest; **model risk = model influence (weight of the model relative to other evidence) combined with decision consequence**; "when no other evidence supports a decision, influence is high"; workflow: define context of use → assess model risk → set credibility goals → plan → execute → decide if credible. Evidence activities: input-data pedigree, software verification, calculation verification, choice of an appropriate comparator, validation against the comparator, uncertainty quantification, sensitivity analysis, applicability to the context of use. Purpose: decide "how much" V&V is necessary. [S] — [Viceconti et al. / PMC6966181](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6966181), [FDA guidance: Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation](https://www.fda.gov/media/175363/download), [ASME V&V 40 overview slides](https://www.imagwiki.nibib.nih.gov/sites/default/files/asme_vv_40_model_simulation_standard.pdf)
- **NASA-STD-7009A** eight factors and 0-4 scale: see section 6. [S] — [NTRS 20230000585](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230000585)
- **PCOLA/SOQ/FAM** (iNEMI): component properties Presence, Correctness, Orientation, Live, Alignment; joint properties Shorts, Opens, Quality; FAM (introduced by iNEMI 2009) for functional tests: Feature, At-speed, Measurement. Each test gets 1 / 0.5 / 0 per property per component. Example: AOI and AXI apply no power, so score 0 on Live. [S] — [ASSET InterTech: PCOLA/SOQ/FAM](https://www.asset-intertech.com/?p=2994), [Optimum Design: PCOLA/SOQ explained](https://www.optimumdesign.com/blogs-and-articles/pcola-soq-explainer/)
- Agilent's Test Coverage Consultant predicted coverage from CAE layout files using PCOLA/SOQ; ASTER TestWay computes theoretical coverage from CAD and measured coverage from test programs. [S] — [Microwaves & RF](https://www.mwrf.com/content/software-checks-test-coverage), [Circuits Assembly](https://circuitsassembly.com/ca/editorial/menu-news/16844-agilent-adds-aster-software-to-testers.html)
- **IPC-9252B** (2016; paywalled) — electrical test of unpopulated boards: continuity (net resistance below limit) and isolation (above limit); direct vs indirect methods; isolation commonly by *adjacency* rather than all-pairs; secondary source gives Level C as 10 Ω max continuity / 10 MΩ min isolation, and test voltage ≥ 40 VDC automated when unspecified; excludes embedded components. Reference data is the IPC-D-356 netlist (ASCII: net names, test-point coordinates, access side, hole/via data). Limitations: does not verify dimensions, mask registration, copper thickness; test current is too low to open a thinned conductor. [S; numeric thresholds from one secondary source] — [pcbsync IPC-9252 summary](https://pcbsync.com/ipc-9252/), [IPC shop listing](https://shop.ipc.org/ipc-9252/ipc-9252-standard-only/Revision-b/english)
- **JLCPCB**: quality page describes bare-board AOI (camera scan after pattern plating / surface finish) and flying-probe electrical test with electro-mechanically controlled probes. A KiCad forum user reports JLCPCB needs no netlist for its flying-probe check: "they compare the Gerbers to the board, based on the copper"; the thread notes that an error already present in the Gerbers is therefore not caught, whereas supplying the true netlist could reveal it. [S; the netlist-source statement is a user report, not JLCPCB documentation] — [JLCPCB quality control](https://jlcpcb.com/about-us/quality-control), [KiCad forum: Verify gerbers with netlist](https://forum.kicad.info/t/verify-gerbers-with-netlist/35700)
- AOI cannot evaluate electrical performance; AXI is the effective method for hidden joints (BGA/QFN/CSP). [S] — [Sierra Circuits: AOI vs AXI vs FPT](https://sierraconnect.protoexpress.com/t/pcb-inspection-methods-aoi-vs-axi-vs-fpt/4129)

### Inferences
- **Apply V&V 40 per claim, in one table**: for each simulation/proof-derived claim record (question of interest; context of use; model influence: is this the only evidence? → high until bench data exists; decision consequence: board respin cost / safety; resulting risk tier; comparator required). Because D1 holds (no bench data), model influence is "high" for every branch-V and branch-VI claim today — which is precisely why the first bench measurements have outsized value: they reduce *influence*, not just add a datapoint.
- **Verification vs validation vs calibration, as used in this project**: *verification* = the checker/solver computes what its spec says (sections 3, 5); *validation* = the model agrees with a comparator (bench) within a stated tolerance for the context of use; *calibration* = adjusting model parameters (e.g. effective copper thickness, εr, solder-joint resistance) to fit bench data — calibration data must not be reused as validation data; with one coupon run, split structures into a calibration set and a held-out validation set before measuring.
- **7009-style scorecard per analysis** (cheap: 8 integers 0-4 in the claim record): input pedigree is where D7 lives (datasheet provenance scheme = pedigree level); referent validation stays 0 until bench; report the minimum.
- **Physical test → branch coverage (PCOLA/SOQ-style, 1 / 0.5 / 0; my assessment)**:

| Evidence source | I fabricable | II assemblable | III copper = schematic | IV footprint = part | V as-built behaviour | VI design correct | VII environment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fab DFM/CAM review accepts order | 0.5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- **Key consequence**: the cheapest single action that converts a fab service into independent evidence for branch III is to generate an IPC-D-356 netlist *from the schematic-level netlist + footprint pad coordinates* (not from the routed copper) and ask the fab to test against it; if the fab will not, compare that IPC-D-356 against the netlist re-extracted from the Gerbers in-house (software-only LVS). Without this, "flying-probe passed" certifies only that the board matches the files.
- **Pin-map errors (the known shared-assumption gap) are invisible to every unpowered test** — AOI, AXI, flying probe and DRC all pass a board whose symbol pin 3 is mapped to the wrong pad. Only (a) an independent pin-map source or (b) a powered functional test sees it. Functional test is therefore mandatory evidence for branch IV, not just VI.
- **Product-development stages, minimal form**: EVT = the coupon/characterisation run (does the chain produce a working board at all; calibrate models); DVT = environment/margin (branch VII); PVT = irrelevant until volume. First-article inspection = the per-footprint fit check and functional test of board #1 before populating the rest. Skip HALT/HASS; substitute supply-voltage and temperature margining with a hair dryer/freezer spray and a bench supply, recorded as `evidence` with the stress range stated.
- **Skip**: IEEE 1012 integrity-level process tables, ICT/boundary-scan fixtures, formal IPC-A-610 class inspection (use its defect *taxonomy* as zoo labels if a free summary is at hand).

## 8. Minimal physical calibration campaign with hobby instruments

### Takeaway
One small "library + chain validation coupon" run (5 boards is the usual minimum order) can retire the highest-value defeaters if it is designed for *coverage of distinct design objects* (every footprint and every template once, with test points) plus *within-board replication of feature classes* (daisy chains, serpentines) to get statistical n in the hundreds — and if predictions with uncertainty are committed to the repo before the boards arrive. Paper 1:1 prints are a valid zero-cost pre-check for coarse-pitch parts only.

### Cited Findings
- **1:1 paper print**: printing the footprint at 1:1 and laying the real part on it is a standard check; caveats — home/office printers do not guarantee true scale (verify with a printed ruler/known dimension); fine-pitch pins cannot be resolved by eye; a 3D-model overlay in CAD is a stronger check of pad position, spacing and outline; manufacturers' own datasheet land patterns are sometimes wrong. [S] — [KiCad forum: Test fitting footprints](https://forum.kicad.info/t/tutorial-test-fitting-footprints/6778), [KiCad forum: how to check footprint correctness](https://forum.kicad.info/t/tutorial-how-to-check-footprint-correctness-pre-version-6-nightlies/29462), [Adafruit Daily: Footprint fail](https://adafruitdaily.com/2024/03/20/footprint-fail)
- **Impedance coupons**: IPC-TM-650 method 2.5.5.7 (free), "Characteristic Impedance of Lines on Printed Boards by TDR" — Z0 from reflected step amplitude; accuracy degrades with distance from the probe launch due to conductor/dielectric loss; references IPC-2141 for design. Vendor rules of thumb (not in the standard): features shorter than ~3 mm are averaged out; coupon length ≥ 150 mm; fab tolerance typically ±10% (±5% premium). [S] — [IPC-TM-650 2.5.5.7A](https://www.electronics.org/sites/default/files/test_methods_docs/2-5-5-7a.pdf), [Atlas PCB coupon guide](https://atlaspcb.com/blog/pcb-impedance-coupon-testing)
- **NanoVNA**: measures S11 and S21 with SOLT calibration; accuracy is set by the calibration standards; an IEEE Microwave Magazine "Educator's Corner" article (Hunt, Scott & Streeter, April 2023) measured the supplied load at ≈ 50.4 Ω at 1 MHz with S11 ≈ -48 dB at low frequency rising to ≈ -35 dB at 1 GHz. [S] — [IEEE Microwave Magazine, April 2023](https://read.nxtbook.com/ieee/microwave/microwave_april_2023/educator_s_corner.html), [NanoVNA user manual](https://nationalmaglab.org/media/0vcoxeen/video4-nanovna-user-manual.pdf)
- **4-wire (Kelvin) resistance**: removes lead/contact resistance by forcing a known current and sensing voltage separately; in one bench demonstration the 2-wire reading was almost twice the 4-wire reading for the same low-value resistor. A battery-powered 4-wire adapter design note reports ≈ ±2% accuracy, limited by the DVM, op-amp offset (±70 µV max) and resistor tolerance; 4-5 digit DVMs with 1 µV sensitivity resolve ~1 µΩ at 1 A. [S] — [Bald Engineer 4-wire tutorial](https://www.baldengineer.com/4-wire-resistance-tutorial.html), [Analog Devices design note](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/design-notes/batterypowered-circuit-measures-milli-ohms-and-micro-ohms.html)
- **Fab electrical test**: certifies shorts/opens against a reference; with JLCPCB the reference is reportedly derived from the Gerbers (user report). [S] — [KiCad forum](https://forum.kicad.info/t/verify-gerbers-with-netlist/35700), [JLCPCB quality control](https://jlcpcb.com/about-us/quality-control)
- **Zero-failure statistics**: 3/n rule (section 6). [S] — [JAMA abstract](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/389714)

### Inferences
- **Stage 0 — zero cost, before any order** (retires part of branch IV and II):
  - Print every library footprint 1:1 with a printed 50 mm calibration bar; measure the bar with calipers; record scale error. Lay real parts on it; photograph; store photo hash in the footprint's provenance record as `evidence: paper-fit`. Valid for ≥ 0.65 mm pitch; for finer pitch record `unknown`.
  - Independent pin-map cross-check against a second library (CERN / manufacturer CAD); record agreement per pin.
  - Run the generated board through KiCad DRC and (if offered) the fab's online DFM checker; keep the reports.
- **Stage 1 — one coupon board design, one 5-board order** (generated *by the Circuit OS pipeline itself*, so the chain is what is being tested):
  1. *Library validation section*: every footprint in the library once, each pad broken out to a labelled test point; half populated (assembly fit), half left bare (pad geometry measurable with calipers/USB microscope). Detects: branch IV, II.
  2. *Template instances with test points*: one each of RC low-pass, resistive divider, LED driver, RS-485 transceiver, regulator — each with input/output/ground test points and a jumper to isolate from supply. Each has a z3/SPICE prediction with tolerance box. Detects: VI, III, IV (pin map), V.
  3. *Daisy chains*: ≥ 300 vias in series, and ≥ 60 instances per pad/joint class (0 Ω resistors in series) with intermediate taps for bisecting a fault. Zero opens on 300 vias → via open rate < ~1% at 95%; 60 joints → < ~5%. Across 5 boards, 1500 vias → < ~0.2% (if boards are exchangeable — same panel/lot is a caveat to state).
  4. *Serpentine traces for 4-wire R*: at each trace width the router uses (e.g. 3 widths × 2 layers), length chosen so R ≈ 0.5-2 Ω (large enough for a ±2% Kelvin adapter at 100 mA-1 A). Predicted R from geometry and nominal copper; measurement calibrates effective copper thickness/etch factor. Hold out one width as validation.
  5. *Clearance/DRC ladder*: comb patterns at rule-minimum, minimum-10%, minimum-20% spacing, each as a separate isolated net pair with test points — tells you where the fab's real limit is relative to the rule deck (and whether the fab silently "fixes" files). Measure isolation with DMM (pass/fail only).
  6. *Net-identity pattern*: a set of nets whose intended connectivity is non-trivial (crossings via layer changes) with all endpoints on a header — a hand continuity matrix (or a microcontroller walking-one test) directly checks copper = schematic for branch III independent of the fab's reference.
  7. *Optional if any controlled-impedance claims are made*: 150 mm 50 Ω microstrip with SMA edge launches at both ends for NanoVNA S11/S21; treat result as screening (±several ohms), `evidence` rung.
  8. *Fiducial dimensions*: two features at a known large spacing (e.g. 80.00 mm) to measure overall scale/registration with calipers.
- **What hobby instruments give** (state the uncertainty in the claim record; figures below are typical and must be replaced with the actual instrument's datasheet values):
  - DMM: DC volts to ~0.5%; 2-wire ohms unusable below ~1 Ω (lead resistance) → use Kelvin adapter; continuity beeper threshold is tens of ohms (not an isolation test).
  - Cheap LCR meter: C and L to a few % at 100 Hz-100 kHz; fixture capacitance of ~1 pF dominates for small C.
  - NanoVNA: relative S11/S21 to ~1 GHz usefully; absolute Z0 only as good as the cal kit.
  - Logic analyser: timing/protocol correctness (RS-485 framing), not edge quality.
  - USB scope: rise time/ringing limited by its bandwidth and probe; qualitative for SI.
  - Not reachable: EMC emissions, real thermal maps, sub-pF parasitics, ppm-level anything. Branch V/VII claims in those areas stay `unknown` with the defeater open — say so.
- **Pre-registration (the step that makes it calibration rather than anecdote)**: before ordering, commit `predictions.yaml` (quantity, predicted value, tolerance, model version hash, acceptance criterion). After measuring, commit `measurements.yaml` (value, instrument, instrument uncertainty, date, operator). A script computes agreement and updates defeater D1 per quantity: refuted-for-this-quantity-class / rebutted (model wrong → new zoo entry + model fix) / inconclusive (instrument uncertainty exceeds tolerance).
- **Outsourced evidence to buy with the order** (cheap relative to a respin): 100% electrical test (usually default — confirm in writing what the reference is); assembly-service AOI for the populated half; X-ray only if the library contains QFN/BGA. Record each as third-party evidence with the fab's own wording and its limitation (section 7 table).
- **What one run does *not* give**: lot-to-lot variation, a second fab's limits, long-term reliability, environment. Word claims as "on one lot from fab X on date Y".
- **Skip**: IPC-2221 conformance coupon sets and microsectioning, thermal-shock coupons, formal Gauge R&R; a single known-value reference resistor/capacitor measured before each session is a sufficient instrument sanity check.

### Gaps
- IPC-2221 Appendix A coupon definitions (paywalled) were not verified; "library validation coupon" as a named professional-librarian practice was not sourced (the 1:1 print and 3D-overlay practices were).
- No sourced uncertainty figures for specific hobby instruments (DMM models, cheap LCR, NanoVNA TDR mode on PCB traces) beyond the NanoVNA load characterisation and the ±2% Kelvin adapter.
- Fab-specific facts (JLCPCB/PCBWay minimum quantities, whether they accept a customer IPC-D-356, AOI/X-ray inclusion and pricing from India, whether they modify Gerbers without notice) were not verified.

## 9. Gates and policy: refuse vs warn, waivers, freshness, attestations

### Takeaway
Make the gate a pure function of (finding severity × rung × waiver state) with exactly three outcomes — refuse, emit-with-residuals, emit-clean — require every waiver to be content-hash-bound and expiring, and bind every piece of evidence to the hashes of the artefact, the rule deck and the checker so that staleness is detected mechanically; the SLSA Verification Summary Attestation format is a ready-made, minimal envelope for the per-board verdict.

### Cited Findings
- **Abort-on-fail gate with version skew risk**: mpw_precheck runs an ordered sequence and aborts on any failure; users warn that the gate's rule deck may differ from the local one. [S] — [mpw_precheck README (snapshot)](https://foss-eda-tools.googlesource.com/third_party/shuttle/sky130/mpw-003/slot-030/+show/82987145c167488d84fae9f226487a9189367251/mpw_precheck/README.md)
- **Regression baseline practice**: first run manually verified and stored as "expected"; re-compared automatically "when the runset changes or a new version of the verification tool becomes available" — i.e. rule-deck change and tool-version change are the named re-verification triggers. [S] — [US 6,606,735](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6606735)
- **KiCad exclusions**: marking a violation as excluded writes an entry to the project's `drc_exclusions`; CLI JSON output with `--severity-all` reports those items with `"excluded": true`; `--severity-exclusions` lists excluded violations; `--exit-code-violations` returns 5 when violations exist; reported defects around exclusions/custom-rule exceptions in CLI vs GUI. [S] — [KiCad 9 CLI reference](https://docs.kicad.org/9.0/en/cli/cli.html), [KiCad issue 24264](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/issues/24264), [KiCad forum](https://forum.kicad.info/t/cli-erc-drc-gives-different-output-than-running-manually-in-gui/55207)
- **SLSA Verification Summary Attestation (VSA)**: an in-toto Statement whose `subject` is the verified artefact identified by digest; predicate type `https://slsa.dev/verification_summary/v1` with fields including `verifier`, `timeVerified`, `resourceUri`, `policy` (uri + digest), `inputAttestations`, `verificationResult`, `verifiedLevels`, and dependency-level summaries; lets a consumer rely on a verification decision without holding all underlying attestations; matching is by `subject.digest`; consumers must ignore unrecognised fields; the predicateType URI changes on backward-incompatible changes. [S] — [SLSA v1.1 VSA spec](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/verification_summary), [in-toto and SLSA](https://slsa.dev/blog/2023/05/in-toto-and-slsa)
- **Tool-confidence determination is per use case** — a change of use invalidates the prior argument. [S] — [SAFECOMP 2012 paper](https://wwwbroy.in.tum.de/~philipps/pub/safecomp2012.pdf)
- **Defeaters must end as refuted or accepted residual risk** (never silently dropped). [S] — [Assurance 2.0 in a Nutshell](https://www.csl.sri.com/~rushby/papers/a2nutshell.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Gate decision table**:

| Finding | Rung of the check | Waiver | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| fail | exact / sound-OA / bounded (decidable) | none | **REFUSE** |
| fail | sound-OA (may be spurious) | valid waiver with justification | emit, listed as waived residual |
| fail | exact | waiver | **REFUSE** unless the *rule* (not the instance) is changed via reviewed rule-deck commit |
| fail | evidence (test/sim) | none | REFUSE by default; configurable to warn per rule |
| unknown (not checked / checker abstained / canary failed) | — | — | emit only with explicit `UNKNOWN` line; REFUSE for rules marked `required` |
| pass | any | — | recorded with rung; never upgrades other claims |

  Additional hard refusals: canary did not fire; rule deck hash ≠ hash the coupons were qualified against; checker hash ≠ qualified hash; any rule with `in_tests = 0`; two diverse channels disagree on a decidable property (disagreement means one is wrong — do not pick one).
- **Waiver record (prevents waiver rot)**: `{rule_id, locus: stable geometric/net identifier, artefact_hash, rule_deck_hash, justification (free text, required), author, created, expires (required, max e.g. 90 days or "next rule-deck change"), defeater_id it leaves open}`. A waiver is void when any bound hash changes — so a re-route automatically re-raises the violation. CI prints waiver count and age; a waiver that matches zero current violations is deleted automatically (stale suppression is as bad as stale evidence). This is the MISRA-deviation / DRC-waiver discipline reduced to a JSON file; never mirror waivers into KiCad's own exclusion list (CLI handling is unreliable per the issue above) — filter KiCad's JSON yourself.
- **Evidence freshness — "verification bill of materials" (VBOM)** per emitted board: `{artefact digests (Gerbers, drill, BOM, PnP, netlist); intent-IR digest; generator versions (git SHA); checker versions + their qualification-suite result digest; rule-deck digest; library part record digests (datasheet record hash + human verification binding); z3/sympy/KiCad versions; per-claim (verdict, rung, evidence digests); waivers; open defeaters}`. Re-verification triggers are then mechanical: any digest in the VBOM that differs from the current one invalidates exactly the claims that depend on it (dependency edges come from the assurance-case pattern). The existing datasheet provenance scheme (human verification bound to record hash) is already this pattern — generalise it to every evidence item.
- **Envelope**: emit the VBOM as an in-toto Statement with the board's fabrication zip digest as `subject`, and a custom predicate (e.g. `https://<project>/board-verification/v1`) modelled on VSA: `verifier` = Circuit OS version, `timeVerified`, `policy` = rule-deck uri + digest, `inputAttestations` = digests of check reports, `verificationResult` = REFUSED / EMITTED-WITH-RESIDUALS / EMITTED-CLEAN, plus the seven-branch vector. Sign it or not — for a one-developer project the unsigned, hash-bound JSON committed next to the output gives the audit trail; signing (Sigstore) can be added later without format change.
- **Reproducibility as a precondition**: evidence bound to a hash is only useful if the pipeline is deterministic from IntentIR down (already the governing rule). Add a CI job that builds the same IntentIR twice and diffs artefact digests; non-determinism (dict ordering, float formatting, timestamps in Gerber headers) is itself a gate failure because it breaks hash-bound evidence.
- **Physical evidence freshness**: bench measurements bind to (footprint record hash, fab name, stack-up/process option, lot/date). A footprint edit voids its paper-fit and coupon evidence; a change of fab voids clearance-ladder and serpentine calibration.
- **Severity vocabulary**: keep it to two orthogonal fields — `verdict ∈ {pass, fail, unknown}` and `policy ∈ {required, advisory}` — rather than error/warning/info, which invites downgrade-to-silence. "Warn" then means exactly "advisory fail or unknown, printed in the residuals".
- **Skip**: SLSA build levels, full in-toto layouts/supply-chain steps, SPDX/CycloneDX SBOM formats, a waiver review board.

### Gaps
- MISRA Compliance:2020 deviation process and commercial EDA waiver-flow documentation (Calibre waiver/Auto-Waivers, KiCad exclusion comments semantics) were not retrieved; the waiver design above is inference by analogy.
- The exact required/optional status of each field in SLSA VSA v1.1 was not verified beyond the search extract; read the spec page before implementing.
- No source found on "evidence staleness" policies specific to hardware design flows.

## 10. AI-specific: how LLM-in-the-loop EDA systems verify outputs (state as of 2026)

### Takeaway
The published consensus pattern is exactly Circuit OS's governing rule — the LLM proposes, an engine (simulator, DRC, subgraph-isomorphism against a datasheet-derived knowledge graph) disposes — and the 2026 PCB benchmarks score generated boards with engine-checked metrics rather than model self-assessment; the gaps the literature itself names are simulation-based verification of generated PCB schematics and semantic (datasheet-level) correctness of connections, which is where Circuit OS's proof + provenance layers are differentiated. No benchmark found measures agreement with physical boards.

### Cited Findings
- **PCB-Bench** (ICLR 2026): ~3,700 expert-annotated text instances, multimodal image-text tasks, and 170+ complete PCB projects (schematics, placement files, documentation); reports "substantial gaps" in models' spatial-placement reasoning, constraint following and interpretation of engineering artefacts. [S] — [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009621)
- **PCBWorld** (arXiv:2607.05915, July 2026): an "engine-grounded" environment on the KiCad engine; agents use DRC feedback to stay within rules; controllable synthetic instances plus 679 real open-source boards; any completed board is scored by eight engine-checked metrics regardless of routing method; agents with engine feedback outperform grid-action RL and open-loop LLM baselines. [S] — [arXiv 2607.05915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05915)
- **OmniRouting** (arXiv:2608.04434, Aug 2026): 1,681 industrial schematic-coupled PCB designs; tasks: geometric routing, design-rule-aware routing, electrical-functionality preservation, tool-augmented agentic routing; current multimodal models show weak path planning, poor adherence to design rules, and inconsistent preservation of electrical functionality. [S] — [alphaXiv 2608.04434](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04434)
- **PCBSchemaGen** (arXiv:2602.00510): states automated PCB schematic design "remains unexplored due to the scarcity of open-source data and the absence of simulation-based verification"; verifies LLM output with a real-datasheet-derived knowledge graph and subgraph isomorphism encoding pin-role semantics and topological constraints. [S] — [arXiv 2602.00510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00510)
- **Agentic EDA survey, "handoff perspective"** (arXiv:2606.19795): for generated PCB content, "pin-map and dimensional correctness, ERC/DRC, routability, and semantic or visual agreement establish technical acceptance." [S] — [arXiv 2606.19795](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19795)
- **Deployed schematic-review agents (DRCY)**: existing EDA tools "enforce structural connectivity rules" but "cannot verify that connections are semantically correct with respect to component datasheets"; system uses a five-agent pipeline, agentic datasheet retrieval with self-evaluation, and multi-run consensus. [S; attribution of this description to arXiv 2603.15672 is from a search extract — verify] — [arXiv 2603.15672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15672)
- **AnalogCoder** (Lai et al., AAAI 2025; arXiv:2405.14918): training-free LLM agent that writes Python (PySpice-style) circuit code, runs simulation, and self-corrects from feedback; stores successful designs as reusable sub-circuit tools; solved 20 benchmark circuits (5 more than plain GPT-4o). [S] — [AAAI proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/32016), [arXiv 2405.14918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14918v1)
- **DRC-Coder** (NVIDIA, 2024): multi-agent LLM generation of DRC code, accepted by F1 = 1.000 agreement with a commercial DRC tool vs 0.631 for standard prompting. [S] — [NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2024-11_drc-coder-automated-drc-checker-code-generation-using-llm-autonomous-agent)
- **Documented failure modes of LLM-generated hardware (RTL)**: FSM mis-sequencing, handshake protocol drift, blocking vs non-blocking misuse, state-space oversimplification; a 2026 taxonomy (syntactic / semantic / solvable functional / unsolvable functional) reports frontier models plateau at 90.8% initial pass rate on VerilogEval, with the plateau defined by unsolvable functional errors; iterative optimisation removes syntax errors while "concurrently exacerbat[ing] deeper functional failures." RAG over tool documentation (RAG-EDA) is used to reduce hallucination. [S] — ["Revolution or Hype? Seeking the Limits of Large Models in Hardware Design", arXiv 2509.04905](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04905v1), [survey preprint 202509.1681](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1681/v1)
- **Supply-side evidence that EDA engines themselves are buggy** (so "the engine said OK" is not ground truth): FUZNET, SynFuzz, VERMEI — see section 4. [S] — [FUZNET](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~afd/papers/2026/FPGA.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Positioning**: the literature's acceptance criteria for generated PCBs are (pin-map + dimensional correctness, ERC/DRC, routability, semantic agreement). Circuit OS adds three things the surveyed systems do not report: per-claim *rungs*, an explicit *defeater register*, and *checker qualification*. None of the benchmarks found reports checker false-negative rates or agreement with fabricated hardware — a publishable differentiator if the coupon campaign is done.
- **Feedback-loop hazard to design against**: the reported "optimisation removes syntax errors but worsens functional failures" result is the generic risk of repair loops that optimise against the checker. Because Circuit OS's LLM only writes IntentIR, the analogous risk is the LLM *editing the intent* until downstream checks pass (weakening a requirement). Mitigations: IntentIR diffs between repair iterations are surfaced to the user as requirement changes; verification obligations are derived deterministically from the *original* accepted intent; the LLM never sees or edits rule thresholds or waivers; cap repair iterations and record the count in the VBOM.
- **Multi-run consensus (DRCY)** is self-agreement of one model — by Knight & Leveson it is weak independence. Use it for *proposing* (datasheet figure extraction candidates), never as a rung above `evidence`; the existing "agent's page-cited evidence + human verification bound to record hash" is the stronger design.
- **Pin-role knowledge graph + subgraph isomorphism (PCBSchemaGen)** is an implementable, deterministic second channel for the pin-map common-mode gap: a separately curated pin-role table (role: VIN / GND / EN / SW / FB …) checked against the generated netlist's topology catches swapped-pin errors that geometry checks cannot.
- **Reusable evaluation harness**: PCBWorld's design (KiCad-engine-checked metrics on any completed board) is the natural external benchmark to run the A* router against, and its 679-board corpus the natural golden corpus (subject to licence check).
- **Mapping LLM failure classes to branches**: hallucinated part numbers/pinouts → IV (provenance scheme); wrong topology or violated datasheet constraint (missing pull-up, absolute-maximum exceeded) → VI (z3 proofs over tolerance boxes); plausible-but-unroutable placement → I/III (router + certificate); "electrical functionality not preserved after routing" (OmniRouting's task 3) → III (LVS from Gerbers).

### Gaps
- Not found / not verified this session: ChipNeMo, VeriGen, AutoChip, LaMAGIC, SPICED, ChipBench, "PCBAgent", "Circuit Mind", Flux Copilot, Quilter, JITX, atopile, tscircuit — no claims about how these verify outputs are made here. A Quilter blog post (quilter.ai/blog/llm-pcb-layout-gpt-6-astra) appeared in results but was not read. Vendor claims about physics validation should be treated as unverified marketing until primary documentation is read.
- No benchmark or paper was found that evaluates LLM-generated PCBs against *fabricated and measured* boards; all found evaluations stop at engine-checked metrics.
- arXiv identifiers for 2026 papers (2607.05915, 2608.04434, 2606.19795, 2603.15672, 2602.00510) are from search extracts; confirm titles/authors before citing in a publication.
- No "LLM4EDA" survey with a PCB-specific failure-mode taxonomy was found; failure modes above are for RTL and transfer to PCB only by analogy.
