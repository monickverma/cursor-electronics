# Check the shipped files, then build one coupon

Circuit OS can validate its boards as strongly as free technology allows by doing three things it does not do today: judging the **fabrication files** instead of the Board IR, judging them with **tools and data that share nothing with the generator**, and **proving on every run that each checker can still see a fault**. Concretely: write a `.kicad_pcb` from the Board IR, let `kicad-cli` act as an independent second design-rule engine and as the exporter, read the Gerbers and drill files back into copper islands and compare them with the netlist, look every part's pinout up by orderable part number in the KiCad symbol library instead of by package, carry board parasitics into the existing z3 proofs as conservative intervals, and emit one hash-bound verdict per board that lists what was proven, what was only tested, and what was not checked. Two pieces were run on this machine during the research and work: `kicad-cli` 10.0.1 produced every export headless, and a 130-line Gerber read-back extractor caught four out of four seeded defects, one of which KiCad's own DRC cannot see. The plan lifts branches I and III to "sound within a stated model", branch IV from "unknown" to "two independent sources agree", and branch V from "unknown" to "bounded"; it leaves EMC, reflow behaviour, the fab's real limits and the model-versus-hardware gap unknown. Those last items need physical evidence, and the minimum is small: a free 1:1 paper fit of every footprint, then one five-board coupon order generated by the pipeline itself, measured with a multimeter and a home-made 4-wire adapter against predictions committed before the boards arrive. Almost all of the pipeline design is the researchers' inference from cited methods; the report marks which parts were run, which were read, and which are open.

## The plan rests on three borrowed ideas, not on new checkers

The decision record for the verifier already names its own weak point: the verifier and the generator share the Board IR and `footprints.PINMAPS`, so a wrong pinout declared there is wrong in both and passes (`.claude/shared-memory/brain/decisions.md`, entry [2026-10-09]). Everything below follows from closing that hole with methods other fields already use.

**First idea: do not verify the generator, check each of its outputs.** Compiler research calls this translation validation: each run is followed by a validation phase that checks the produced output against the source, because proving the compiler itself is too hard and must be redone on every change ([Pnueli, Siegel and Singerman, TACAS 1998](https://doi.org/10.1007%2FBFb0054170)). The algorithmic version is the certifying algorithm, which hands back with each answer a witness that a much simpler program can check ([McConnell, Mehlhorn, Näher and Schweitzer, 2011](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/certifying-algorithms/)). CompCert, the formally verified C compiler, uses exactly this for register allocation: the allocator is untrusted heuristic code and a small validator checks every result ([Rideau and Leroy](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~rideau/docs/papers/RideauLeroy.pdf)). For Circuit OS this means the router, placer, footprint generator and LLM stay untrusted for good. What has to be right is a short list: the small checkers, the file parsers, the rule numbers, and z3. That list is the trusted base, and the plan keeps it small on purpose.

**Second idea: copy how chips are signed off.** The open chip flow OpenLane 2 signs a design off with a tool different from the one that built it, and often with two: it writes the layout with both Magic and KLayout and XORs the two files, runs design rules in both engines, extracts a netlist from the layout and compares it with the schematic, and extracts parasitics for timing ([OpenLane 2 flows reference](https://openlane2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/flows.html)). Foundry rule decks are themselves tested: the IHP open PDK keeps a passing and a failing test structure per rule and passes a rule only when it reports no false positive and no false negative, with flags showing that every rule in the deck is also in the tests ([IHP Open-PDK DRC testing](https://ihp-open-pdk-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/verification/drc/04_05_testing.html)). And the shuttle precheck is a fixed, ordered list of checks that aborts on the first failure ([mpw_precheck README](https://foss-eda-tools.googlesource.com/third_party/shuttle/sky130/mpw-003/slot-030/+show/82987145c167488d84fae9f226487a9189367251/mpw_precheck/README.md)). The PCB translation is direct: KiCad DRC is the second engine, the Gerber read-back is the layout-versus-schematic step, closed-form parasitics are the extraction, and `Report.gate()` becomes the precheck.

**Third idea: independence has to come from different inputs, not from writing the same check twice.** In the classic experiment, 27 programs written independently from one specification failed together far more often than independence predicts, because people err on the same hard parts ([Knight and Leveson, 1986](https://www.kth.se/social/files/564df871f2765419e306178d/KnightLeveson.pdf)). A second in-house checker reading the same Board IR would buy little. A checker reading the emitted Gerbers, or a pinout taken from a library someone else transcribed, buys a lot. The tool-qualification standards reach the same place from the other side: under ISO 26262 a tool whose errors are reliably caught downstream needs no qualification at all, and the judgement is made per use, not per tool ([Hillebrand et al., SAFECOMP 2012](https://wwwbroy.in.tum.de/~philipps/pub/safecomp2012.pdf)). So the generators need no qualification once their outputs are checked, and the checkers are what must be tested.

One vocabulary carries through the whole report. The five rungs already in `verify.py` stay: exact, sound, bounded, evidence, unknown. The plan adds one field the rungs lack, which the researchers call **model validity**: every rung is true about a model, and "exact on the model, model not validated against hardware" is the honest reading of every board today. Evidence in this report is marked the same way throughout: **run** means executed on this machine, **read** means the researchers opened the primary page, **summary** means taken from a search-result extract and not opened, and **inference** means the researchers' or this report's own reasoning.

## Nine stages take a Board IR to a per-board verdict

The table gives the whole flow. Each stage either refuses, passes with a rung, or reports unknown; nothing is skipped silently. The stage order is the researchers' design, built from the cited pieces.

| # | Stage | Tool or mechanism | What it produces | Refuses when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Part truth | KiCad symbol and footprint libraries read from the install; datasheet pinout records (D7 pattern) | Pin map and pad geometry per orderable part number, with a trust level | No pin map resolves; two sources disagree; a footprint only matches mirrored |
| 2 | Board IR checks | `verify.py` and the DRC kernel (existing) | Connectivity, shorts, courtyards, on the IR | Any exact failure (as today) |
| 3 | KiCad project writer | Own s-expression emitter for `.kicad_pcb`, `.kicad_pro`, `.kicad_dru`; `kicad-cli pcb upgrade --force` | A board KiCad itself has re-saved | KiCad cannot load or re-save it |
| 4 | Second rule engine | `kicad-cli pcb drc`, JSON output, sentinel rule, policy table | Violations from a different geometry kernel | Sentinel missing; any required rule fires; the two engines disagree on a decidable property |
| 5 | Fab and assembly rules | Fab profile as data; own geometric checks for what KiCad lacks | DFM and DFA findings against published limits | A required limit is broken |
| 6 | Export | `kicad-cli pcb export gerbers / drill / pos / ipcd356 / ipc2581` | The files that would be ordered | Any export exits non-zero |
| 7 | Read-back comparison | Gerbonara + Shapely extractor in its own interpreter; KLayout as test oracle | Copper islands from the Gerbers, compared with the netlist by pad position | An open, a short, a pad not on copper, a pad label that disagrees with the IR |
| 8 | As-built bounds | Closed-form R, L, C as intervals into the z3 prover; geometric rules for decoupling, current, creepage, return path | Post-layout proofs labelled bounded | A graded quantity leaves tolerance inside the interval |
| 9 | Verdict | Assurance-case pattern, weakest-rung rule, hash-bound record | REFUSED, EMITTED WITH RESIDUALS, or EMITTED CLEAN, plus a seven-branch vector | Any stage refused; a canary did not fire |

### Stage 1: pinouts come from the part number, never from the package

This is the highest-value change and needs no new dependency. The researchers parsed the KiCad 10.0.1 libraries installed on this machine with a throwaway 25-line tokenizer (**run**): 222 symbol libraries, **22,728 symbols, 79% with a default footprint**, each carrying pin number, pin name and a datasheet link; and 15,430 footprints, 78% of them script-generated ([kicad-symbols](https://gitlab.com/kicad/libraries/kicad-symbols), [kicad-footprints](https://gitlab.com/kicad/libraries/kicad-footprints)). Grouping symbols by footprint gives the number that settles the design: **one SOT-23 footprint carries 39 different pin assignments across 334 parts, TO-92 carries 30, SOT-223 carries 15, and SOIC-8 carries 298**. The project's SOT-23 map (1 = base or gate, 2 = emitter or source, 3 = collector or drain) matches 113 of those 334 parts and is wrong for every regulator, reference, diode and sensor in that package. Regulators alone come in four orders in SOT-23. In TO-92 the library shows a concrete trap: an MCP1700 is ground, input, output while a 78L05 or LP2950 is output, ground, input, so dropping one into the other's footprint reverses the regulator. The same reading shows `footprints.py` maps TO-92 as emitter, base, collector, which fits a 2N3904 and is the reverse of a BC547 in the KiCad library (this report's own reading of `backend/pcb_engine/footprints.py` against the researchers' table).

The mechanism is a lookup chain, tried in order: exact manufacturer part number in the KiCad symbol library; then KiCad's wildcard names (the library writes `MCP1700x-330xxTT`, so a small pattern matcher is needed); then a hand-authored, page-cited datasheet pinout record in the existing D7 style; then refuse. The parser must follow `extends` chains, which run two deep for the MAX485E. Three holes in the library were seen and need handling: blank pin names (the SRD relay, BAT54A/C, op-amp outputs), empty default footprints for multi-package parts (MAX485E, LM358), and unnormalised names (VI, VIN, IN), which need a synonym table. A DHT22 symbol was not confirmed to exist; only the DHT11 was found. Modules and relays therefore need hand-written records.

Trust is recorded on two short ladders the researchers propose (**inference**). For pin maps: T0 is today's package map or free-pad fallback; T1 is agreement with the KiCad symbol for the exact part number; T2 adds an agent-extracted datasheet pin table with page citation that also agrees; T3 adds a person's sign-off bound to the record hash. For geometry: G0 is pad count only (today); G1 is pad count, pitch and row span matching the KiCad footprint; G2 adds a lead-coverage check against datasheet package dimensions; G3 is human-verified. Every `land_patterns.json` entry can move from `verified: false` to G1 mechanically, with `source: kicad-footprints@<commit>`. A disagreement between the KiCad symbol and the datasheet record fails closed and shows both citations; that event is the SOT-23 class of bug being caught.

Geometry comparison has to be written; `kicad-cli fp` offers only export and upgrade (**run**). The proposed algorithm is small: centre both footprints on the centroid of their pads, require the same pad numbers, try the eight rotations and mirrors, and match pads by number. A match that only works **mirrored is a hard failure**, since that is the bottom-view drawing mistake common on TO-92 parts, relays and modules; a match that only works rotated is a warning. Repeating the match while ignoring pad numbers separates "geometry right, numbering wrong" from "geometry wrong" and reports the exact swap. For the check against the real part, the IPC land-pattern equations are public and can be inverted: the pad row must bracket the lead span, and the toe, heel and side fillets achieved by the footprint are compared with thresholds held as cited parameters ([IPC presentation, Bergman 2004](https://www.electronics.org/system/files/technical_resource/E31%26S01-3_Dieter.pdf)). The full IPC-7351B tables are paywalled and were not obtained; the only table values retrieved are the 2004 ones, and they must be stored with that label.

One convention must be fixed now. KiCad numbers a diode's cathode as pin 1 (1N4148W and 1N4007 both read 1 = K, 2 = A in the installed library). `footprints.py` uses cathode = pad 1 for SOD-123 but anode = pad 1 for DO-41 and for the two-terminal chip packages. That is self-consistent inside Circuit OS, and it will reverse a diode the moment a board is compared with, or exported onto, a KiCad footprint (this report's reading of the file; not run). Adopt the KiCad convention everywhere and add a test.

The licence allows all of this. The KiCad libraries are CC-BY-SA 4.0 with an exception that waives the share-alike terms for designs and generated files; the exception does not cover redistributing the library, or parts of it, as a collection ([KiCad library licence](https://www.kicad.org/libraries/license/)). Reading the libraries from a pinned install at run time and recording the commit and file hash is clean. Serving a parsed copy to users would make that data a separately licensed CC-BY-SA artefact. The commercial model aggregators are not usable as a stored second source: SnapMagic's own staff say some footprints are linked to parts by package without a datasheet check ([SnapEDA Q&A](https://www.snapeda.com/questions/question/wrong-footprint-4/)), and the EasyEDA library has reported pin errors on exactly the SRD relay family this project uses ([EasyEDA forum](https://www.easyeda.com/forum/topic/RELAY-SL-SRD-schematic-and-PCB-pin-orientations-are-WRONG-f246680ca9f044a19ae35ff494763595)).

### Stages 3 and 4: KiCad becomes the second design-rule engine

Write the `.kicad_pcb` as plain text from the Board IR. The alternatives are closed for now: KiCad's new Python API needs a running GUI on versions 9 and 10 and has no export calls, the old SWIG bindings are planned for removal in KiCad 11 ([KiCad developer docs](https://dev-docs.kicad.org/en/apis-and-binding/index.html), summary), and `kiutils` is GPL-3.0 with no stated support for KiCad 9 or 10 ([kiutils](https://github.com/mvnmgrx/kiutils)). The subset needed is small, and it was shown to work here: hand-edited boards with segments removed and inserted by text splice loaded and exported without complaint, the header reads `(version 20260206)`, and nets are referenced by name string rather than by index (**run**). Round-trip every written board through `kicad-cli pcb upgrade --force` so KiCad rewrites it in its own form, and treat a failure there as a writer bug. Net classes and severities live in the sibling `.kicad_pro` and custom rules in a `.kicad_dru` with the same base name; without them KiCad silently runs its defaults.

Run `kicad-cli pcb drc --format json --severity-all --all-track-errors --refill-zones --exit-code-violations` on a scratch copy ([KiCad 10 CLI docs](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/cli/cli.html)). Observed exit codes (**run**): 0 for clean, 5 for violations, 3 when the board fails to load. Parse the JSON anyway and treat any other exit code as an infrastructure failure. Four traps were found or read and each has a fixed answer.

A broken custom-rule file is dropped without a word: KiCad applies custom rules only if the file has no errors, and one unrecognised layer name makes `kicad-cli` report nothing ([kicad-druid README](https://github.com/Cimos/kicad-druid), read). The answer is a **sentinel rule**: an assertion that must always fire, appended to every generated rule file; the gate requires its violation in the JSON and reports "rules not loaded" if it is missing. Its interaction with KiCad's last-match-wins rule order was not tested on 10.0.1 and should be the first thing tried.

Several checks that matter are not errors by default. KiCad's source sets copper sliver, connection width, hole-to-hole and the silkscreen checks to warning and sets missing courtyard and footprint type mismatch to ignore ([board_design_settings.cpp](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/master/pcbnew/board_design_settings.cpp), read). Do not rely on KiCad's severities; keep a policy table in Circuit OS keyed by KiCad's machine names and decide required or advisory there.

`--schematic-parity` is useless without a schematic: the command prints a warning, writes an empty parity list, and exits as if parity passed (**run**). Skip it and compare against the IR netlist directly.

Stored exclusions and baseline noise distort counts: the unmodified demo board reported hole-clearance, starved-thermal and silk findings once `--severity-all` bypassed its exclusions (**run**). Filter by violation type, never by total count, and keep waivers in Circuit OS, not in KiCad's exclusion list.

The differential harness compares the in-house kernel with KiCad on the same boards and sorts each disagreement into kernel miss, kernel false alarm, KiCad miss, or a documented difference in rule meaning. It has a ready acceptance test. The earlier router report executed a real defect in the kernel: its broad phase pads boxes by track width while the narrow phase needs clearance plus half-widths, so two parallel 0.2 mm tracks at 0.25 mm clearance and 0.40 to 0.44 mm separation are genuine violations the kernel does not report (`reports/Validating the router in software.md`, run in that round). Build the harness first, watch it catch that bug, then fix the bug.

### Stage 5: fab limits are data, and KiCad's blind spots are a short list

Hold fab limits as one data file per fab with a source link and access date per number, and generate both the `.kicad_dru` and the native rule table from it, with a drift check in CI. That is the kicad-druid pattern (MIT): capability files generate the rule files, a linter checks them, and CI runs `kicad-cli` on test boards that deliberately contain passing and failing footprints ([kicad-druid](https://github.com/Cimos/kicad-druid)). Use kicad-druid's numbers as absolute fab limits, not as targets: its JLCPCB two-layer file encodes a via annular ring of 0.05 mm ([JLCPCB-2L-1oz.kicad_dru](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cimos/kicad-druid/main/JLCPCB/JLCPCB-2L-1oz.kicad_dru)), while JLCPCB's own page recommends 0.25 mm and gives 0.18 mm as the floor for plated-hole pads ([JLCPCB capabilities](https://jlcpcb.com/capabilities/pcb-capabilities)).

The researchers read the capability pages of JLCPCB, PCBWay, OSH Park and Lion Circuits directly, and Aisler's rules from its community post, on 2026-10-09 and derived a portable two-layer profile by taking the weakest fab per parameter and rounding up (the profile is **inference**; the inputs are **read**). The main values: track and gap 0.20 mm with 0.25 mm default routing; via drill 0.40 mm in a 0.80 mm pad; plated-hole ring 0.25 mm; hole to hole 0.50 mm; hole to foreign copper 0.35 mm; copper to board edge 0.40 mm; solder-mask web 0.16 mm; silk line 0.16 mm and text 1.0 mm; 1.6 mm board, 1 oz copper; no castellations. For an India-based order the relevant local figures are Lion Circuits' standard tier: 6/6 mil track and gap, 0.35 mm minimum drill, 0.152 mm annular ring, 0.3 mm copper to edge, 0.152 mm mask dam ([Lion Circuits capabilities](https://lioncircuits.com/pcb-manufacturing-capabilities)); the portable profile sits inside all of them. Its rush tier is looser-capable (8/8 mil, 0.4 mm drill) and needs its own overlay.

Compute the annular ring at worst case, not nominal. Fabs differ on whether the stated drill is the finished hole or the tool, and JLCPCB publishes a hole position tolerance of ±0.075 mm and a plated-hole size tolerance of +0.13 mm, so a designed 0.18 mm ring can finish near 0.04 mm; the 0.25 mm figure leaves about 0.11 mm (researchers' arithmetic on the read figures). This check is "bounded": it stands on published tolerances, not measured ones.

KiCad's rule list, read from its source, has no paste or stencil check, no acid-trap check as such, no same-net mask-web check, no pick-and-place rotation check and no thermal-symmetry check ([drc_item.cpp](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/master/pcbnew/drc/drc_item.cpp)); the blind-spot list is derived from absence, not from a KiCad statement. These are the checks Circuit OS writes itself on Shapely polygons: acute copper angles; slivers by shrinking and re-growing the copper; mask web between adjacent openings; via inside an SMD pad; pad thermal symmetry on two-pad parts (a heuristic for tombstoning, so evidence rung); and component body to board edge of 2.5 mm when JLCPCB assembles ([JLCPCB assembly terms](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/Terms-and-Conditions-of-JLCPCB-Assembly-Service), summary). For the placement file, generate it natively from the IR, then recompute where pin 1 lands from the file as an assembler would read it and compare with where the board has pad 1. Rotation cannot be fully proven from public data: KiBot's own documentation says rotation depends on tape orientation, that its correction table is "just a list of common cases", and that two parts with the same footprint can need different rotations ([KiBot position-file notes](https://kibot.readthedocs.io/en/latest/notes_position.html), read). So the fab's placement preview stays a recorded human step. Fab web DFM checkers are the same: no fab offers one that can run in CI, so an upload result is attached to the release as evidence.

### Stages 6 and 7: read the Gerbers back and compare them with the netlist

Export with `kicad-cli`, which wrote Gerber X2, Excellon, IPC-D-356, IPC-2581, ODB++ and the position file from a bare board on this machine (**run**). Use `--excellon-separate-th` or Gerber-format drill so plating is explicit, pass `--units mm` to the position export because its default is inches, and set no auxiliary origin in the written board so every file shares one frame.

The read-back extractor was prototyped and run (**run**, about 130 lines, on KiCad's bundled `pic_programmer` demo: two layers, 244 plated holes, 4 zones, 111 nets). It parses each copper Gerber with Gerbonara, turns every dark object into a Shapely polygon, unions each layer into islands, joins islands across layers wherever a plated hole touches copper on both, then places every pad label in an island. A net spread over more than one island is an open; an island holding more than one net is a short. It took about 1.1 seconds.

| Board | KiCad DRC | Own extractor | KLayout extractor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean | 0 violations | 0 opens, 0 shorts | 0 / 0 |
| One track segment deleted | unconnected item, exit 5 | open on that net | same |
| One via deleted | unconnected item | open on that net | same |
| Bridge added between two nets | shorting items | short between those nets | same |
| One drill hit moved 3 mm in the drill file only | **cannot see it** | open on that net | same |

The last row is the reason this stage exists: a fault in the exported files is invisible to every check that reads the board. The same run proved the plan's caution about labels. KiCad 10.0.1 writes IPC-D-356 net names cut to their **last 14 characters** with no alias table, and in a frame shifted by the auxiliary origin; with the wrong frame, 150 of 253 records landed off copper and the rest produced a false 50-net short (**run**). So the reference for the comparison is the IR's own pad table (reference, pin, net, position, side), the X2 pad attributes in the Gerber are a second label set that checks the writer, and the IPC-D-356 is never keyed on. The comparison is a test that two groupings of pads are the same grouping; graph matching is not needed when both sides have pad positions.

Three limits of the prototype must be stated with it. It ran on Python 3.13, on one mostly through-hole board, with four mutations. Gerbonara 1.6.3 could not read net attributes on region objects and could not tell plated from non-plated holes in KiCad's Excellon comments (**run**). And the prototype's hole test is too generous. Before this stage carries more than the evidence rung it needs the guards the researchers listed (**inference**): plating taken from the file header; a hole joins only the island its centre lies in, with a second island inside the disc reported as breakout; a ring of copper must remain around the hole; non-plated holes are cut out of the copper and never join layers; clear-polarity objects are applied in file order or the file is refused; islands are clipped to the board outline; arcs are handled with a bounded chord error. Near-misses (tangent contact, grazing drills, gaps under 10 µm) were not tested and are where a geometric extractor is most likely to be wrong.

Gerbonara is Apache-2.0 and actively released, but **it will not install on Python 3.11**: `pip download gerbonara==1.6.3 --python-version 3.11` failed (**run**; [gerbonara on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/gerbonara/)). Run the read-back stage in its own Python 3.12 interpreter or container. That also keeps it at arm's length from the code that made the board. KLayout's Python wheel, driven through a small XML project file, reproduced every result in about 2.8 seconds per board (**run**); it is GPL-3.0-or-later, so use it as a test-time oracle in a separate process, and guard against its documented silent failure where a drill file imports no vias at all ([KLayout forum](https://www.klayout.de/forum/discussion/comment/4450)) by asserting the via count. No open tool was found that does this comparison end to end; the nearest is an open, unimplemented feature request proposing the same design ([delta-gerber issue #25](https://github.com/Delta-Proto/delta-gerber/issues/25)).

### Stage 8: parasitics enter the proofs as intervals, and no field solver is needed

The earlier report planned PyPEEC plus a capacitance extractor. The new notes change that recommendation. For this board class every per-trace parasitic sits two or more orders of magnitude below component tolerance, so the useful output is a recorded proof that it is negligible, plus the rare flagged exception. The researchers' arithmetic, from one cited formula each: 1 oz copper is about 0.49 mΩ per square, so a 0.25 mm by 15 mm trace is about 29 mΩ; a 1.6 mm via is about 1.2 nH by the Johnson and Graham formula ([Signal Consulting](https://www.sigcon.com/Pubs/news/6_04.htm), summary); a 50 mm trace over a far-side pour is about 2 pF and 39 nH.

The mechanism (**inference**; no published precedent was searched for) fits the existing prover without change. Each parasitic becomes one more parameter in the tolerance box, ranging from zero to a conservative upper bound: the closed-form value inflated by a margin that covers etch, dielectric constant, thickness and temperature. Because zero is inside the range, the ideal circuit is a member of the box and the post-layout proof strictly contains the pre-layout one. A defensible margin is ±20 to 30% on any capacitance or inductance, since input uncertainty dominates the formula's own fit error. Resistance stays rational, so z3 handles DC quantities unchanged; capacitance adds to existing capacitor terms; series inductance is dismissed by a separate bound rather than carried into z3. A cheap pre-filter records "negligible by bound" and skips the parameter whenever its share is under a tenth of the remaining slack. One open tool does something similar by injecting trace resistance and via inductance into test benches and reports a 48 mΩ trace moving an RC cutoff by 0.3% ([kicad-happy spice skill](https://skills.sh/aklofas/kicad-happy/spice), summary). The result is labelled bounded.

PyPEEC remains a real option later: it is pure Python, takes Gerber, and returns resistance and inductance, but it models no capacitance and no dielectrics ([PyPEEC on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pypeec)), and a floating-point solver output cannot be a proof premise on its own. The DC drop solver padne is GPL-3.0 and Linux-oriented, and its measurement validation was still future funded work in April 2026 ([padne](https://github.com/atx/padne), [NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/project/Padne/)); a per-net squares count with a 2% threshold does the job here. Antmicro's gerber2ems is built for multi-gigahertz interfaces and does not simulate capacitors ([gerber2ems](https://github.com/antmicro/gerber2ems), read); it is out of class.

The rest of branch V is rules on the Board IR, each labelled with where its threshold comes from. Decoupling: Microchip's device documentation asks for the trace from pin to capacitor to be within 6 mm ([Microchip decoupling guidance](https://onlinedocs.microchip.com/oxy/GUID-7D545F23-E6CC-4AB1-9E35-ED2CB0ADA187-en-US-3/GUID-FD97F122-F9A6-43B5-B877-2E574000E001.html), summary); that one threshold is citable, and the loop-length and via-count rules derived from it are heuristics. Current: IPC-2221's formula gives about 0.88 A for a 0.25 mm outer trace at 10 °C rise (researchers' arithmetic on the formula as reproduced by [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/ipc-2221-calculator-pcb-trace-current-and-heating)), far above any logic-side current on these boards. The exception is the relay contact path carrying mains load, which never appears in the low-voltage netlist and needs its own rule keyed on the relay's contact rating. Creepage for the mains relay: the commonly reproduced basic-insulation figure at 250 V, pollution degree 2, is **2.5 mm** ([Skyworks AN583](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/SkyWorks/SL/documents/public/application-notes/AN583.pdf), [Analog Devices](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/technical-articles/comparative-tracking-index-and-working-voltage.html)), twice the 1.25 mm that IPC-2221B gives for uncoated outer copper ([Sierra Circuits](https://protoexpress.com/blog/ipc-2221-circuit-board-design)); a tool checking only the IPC figure would pass an unsafe layout. All three sources are summaries; the safety standards themselves are paywalled and were not read. KiCad has a creepage rule, but users report missed violations near slots and results that change between runs ([KiCad forum](https://forum.kicad.info/t/creepage-drc-does-not-work/59857)), so compute creepage in-house as the shortest surface path around slots and use KiCad only as a cross-check. EMC stays out of scope and is said so: the small-loop and cable formulas give order-of-magnitude budgets only, and the inputs they need are not in the Board IR.

### Stage 9: the verdict is a generated argument, not a score

The existing claims and defeater register are already an informal assurance case. Adopt the vocabulary of Assurance 2.0 and its one hard rule: a doubt is either refuted by evidence or accepted as a named residual risk, never dropped ([Bloomfield, Netkachova and Rushby, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15800)); and confidence is reported from three sides, what is supported, what doubts were raised and refuted, and what doubts remain ([Rushby and Bloomfield](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04522)). In practice the seven-branch tree is written once by hand as a pattern and kept in version control; each run fills its leaves from the findings, and any leaf with no finding becomes an explicit "not developed" entry. A JSON file is the normative record. The MIT-licensed gsn2x renders such a tree from YAML to SVG ([gsn2x](https://github.com/jonasthewolf/gsn2x)); what it validates was not confirmed, so treat it as a drawing tool only.

Rungs combine by the weakest link and are never averaged, which is the rule NASA's modelling standard uses for input data: the lowest quality represents the whole set ([NASA NTRS 20090005963](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20090005963/downloads/20090005963.pdf), summary). Two agreeing evidence-grade checks do not make a bounded one; they retire the specific shared-assumption doubt they address. The gate is a fixed table (**inference**): a decidable failure refuses; a failure at the exact rung cannot be waived, only the rule can be changed by a reviewed commit; an unknown on a rule marked required refuses; an advisory unknown is printed. Extra hard refusals: the canary did not fire, the rule file hash differs from the one the test coupons were qualified against, or two engines disagree on a decidable property. Waivers are records bound to the artefact hash and rule hash, with a required reason and expiry, so a re-route re-raises the violation by itself.

Each emitted board carries a "verification bill of materials": digests of the Gerbers, drill, BOM, placement file and netlist; generator and checker versions; rule-file hash; part-record hashes; KiCad and z3 versions; per-claim verdict and rung; waivers; open doubts. The SLSA verification summary format is a ready-made envelope for exactly this kind of record ([SLSA VSA spec](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/verification_summary), summary); an unsigned, hash-bound JSON beside the output is enough for one developer. The text of the verdict is generated from a template and linted so the words "verified", "correct" and "works" never appear without a rung, and it always ends with the sentence that the board has not been built or measured, until it has.

### The checkers earn their rung by being tested, every run

A pass from a checker counts for nothing until the checker has been shown to fire. The qualification suite is the foundry discipline plus standard software testing, applied by inference (no published method for PCB checkers was found). Per rule: generated test boards just inside and just outside the threshold, with the IHP-style flags so that a rule without a test fails CI. Per production run: a **canary**, one known violation per rule family injected into a scratch copy, which must be reported; this is what turns "0 violations" from ambiguous into meaningful. Nightly: mutation testing of the checker code itself with Cosmic Ray, which flags any code change the tests fail to notice ([EuroPython 2024](https://ep2024.europython.eu/session/mutation-testing-in-python-with-cosmic-ray)). Continuously: seeded defects applied to boards and to fab files (cut a segment, delete a via, bridge two nets, move a drill hit, move a hit from the plated to the non-plated file, swap or mirror a layer, swap two pins), each labelled with the rule that must fire; equivalence changes that must not move the verdict (precision 5 instead of 6, Gerber-format drill, whole-board rotation); and random small boards built with known connectivity using Hypothesis, which shrinks any failure to a minimal example ([Hypothesis](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/)). Every bug ever found goes into an append-only folder of known-bad boards before its fix is merged. An import rule in CI forbids checker modules from importing generator modules. Recall measured on seeded defects is an upper-ish bound, because seeds come from defect classes already thought of; that sentence stays attached to every recall figure.

## The minimum physical evidence: paper first, then one coupon

Software reaches the files. It does not reach the parts, the fab or the bench. The smallest evidence that closes those gaps comes in two stages (the campaign design is **inference**; the individual practices are cited).

**Stage 0 costs nothing.** Print every library footprint at 1:1 with a 50 mm calibration bar, measure the bar with calipers, lay the real part on the print, photograph it, and store the photo's hash in the footprint's record. Printing a footprint and laying the part on it is a standard community check, with the known caveats that office printers do not guarantee scale and fine pitch cannot be judged by eye ([KiCad forum tutorial](https://forum.kicad.info/t/tutorial-test-fitting-footprints/6778), summary). It is valid for the pitches this project uses and must be recorded as unknown for anything finer than about 0.65 mm.

**Stage 1 is one coupon board, one five-board order, generated by Circuit OS itself**, so that the chain is the thing under test. It carries: every footprint in the library once, each pad broken out to a test point, half populated and half bare; one instance of each generator's circuit (RC low-pass, divider, LED driver, DHT22 node, RS-485 node, load switch) with input, output and ground test points and an isolating jumper; daisy chains of at least 300 vias and at least 60 joints per pad class with taps; serpentine traces at each routed width and layer, long enough to read 0.5 to 2 Ω; comb patterns at the rule minimum and at 10% and 20% below it; a net-identity pattern whose endpoints all reach a header so a hand continuity matrix checks copper against schematic without trusting the fab's reference; and two marks a known distance apart for scale.

The repetition is there because five boards say almost nothing on their own. With zero failures in n trials the 95% upper bound on the failure rate is roughly 3/n ([Hanley and Lippman-Hand, JAMA 1983](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/389714)); the exact form gives about 45% for five boards, 5% for 59 trials and 1% for 299 (researchers' derivation). Changing the unit of trial from board to feature is what makes one order useful: 300 vias with no open bounds the via open rate near 1%. Repetition measures the process, though, not the design: 300 copies of a wrong footprint fail 300 times. Design correctness needs each distinct object once, which is why every footprint and every template appears.

Before ordering, commit a predictions file: quantity, predicted value, tolerance, model hash, acceptance rule. After measuring, commit the measurements with instrument and uncertainty. A script then marks the bench doubt (D1) per quantity as refuted, contradicted, or inconclusive. Measured values used to tune a model parameter must not be reused to validate it, so hold one trace width out. For the instruments: 2-wire ohms on a multimeter is unusable at these values, and a 4-wire adapter of the kind Analog Devices describes reaches about ±2% ([Analog Devices design note](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/design-notes/batterypowered-circuit-measures-milli-ohms-and-micro-ohms.html), summary). Hobby gear will not reach EMC emissions, thermal maps or sub-picofarad parasitics; those stay unknown and the verdict says so. One run also says nothing about a second lot or a second fab, so claims are worded "one lot, this fab, this date". A footprint edit voids its paper fit; a change of fab voids the comb and serpentine results.

## Conclusion

The earlier reports asked how far software can go and answered with a tree. This round changes what "go further" means. It is not more checks on the Board IR; the IR is already judged exactly. It is moving the judge to the other side of the export, and feeding it facts the generator never wrote. One drill hit moved in one file, missed by KiCad and caught by a 130-line script, is the whole argument in miniature. The same logic explains the reversed recommendation on field solvers: on these boards a solver would compute, expensively, numbers that are already known to be negligible, while the part's pinout, the thing most likely to kill a board silently, was being read from a table the generator shares.

The second shift is in what a verdict is. A board will not come out "correct". It will come out with seven lines, each carrying a rung and its open doubts, bound by hash to the exact files, rules and checker versions that produced them. That record is also what makes the coupon cheap: with predictions committed first, five small boards and a multimeter turn the standing phrase "nothing has been built on a bench" into a per-quantity measurement, and none of the published AI board-design benchmarks found in this research reports agreement with fabricated hardware at all.
