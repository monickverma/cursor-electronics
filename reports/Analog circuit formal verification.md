# Bounds, not compute, make analog proofs tractable

Formal verification of analog circuits is hard for three different reasons, and only one of them responds to more hardware. Some questions are **provably impossible**: unbounded reachability for linear hybrid automata is undecidable, and so is deciding sign conditions on expressions that mix exp and sin. Some are **provably expensive**: polynomial real arithmetic is decidable but doubly exponential in the worst case, and stability of cubic ODEs is strongly NP-hard. Most of the rest is **hard in practice**, because over-approximations drift loose over long horizons, grids explode with dimension, and every proof covers only a model of the circuit. Undecidability cannot be solved with more compute, because no algorithm exists at any budget. What makes progress possible is restricting the question: fix a model, bound the parameters and the time horizon, pick a decidable or semi-decidable fragment, and accept a sound "don't know" as a valid outcome. That is good news for Circuit OS. The five Phase 1 templates (divider, RC low-pass, LED plus resistor, DHT22 and MAX485 boards with the MCU modelled as a 100 Ω load) are algebraic or first-order linear systems, and exact proofs over full component tolerance ranges are available today in Python with sympy and z3, in milliseconds. Linear op-amp stages stay in cheap, decidable territory. Saturation, comparators and switching power stages are where the impossibility results start to apply and the work becomes a research effort. On novelty, every step of the proposed "LLM → goal → solver → run → interpret → refine" loop already has published precedent for generic continuous and hybrid systems. The searches found no LLM analog-design agent that uses a formal engine, so the defensible claim is a first integration for analog circuits, with the LLM kept outside the trusted core, not a new verification method.

## Three different walls: impossible, expensive, and merely hard

It helps to separate the obstacles by the kind of result behind them, because each calls for a different response. A theorem of impossibility means the question has to be changed. A complexity lower bound means the problem has to stay small. A practical obstacle means the method has to be chosen and tuned well.

### Provably impossible: questions no algorithm answers

The sharpest boundary comes from hybrid automata, the standard model for circuits whose behaviour switches between modes. Henzinger, Kopke, Puri and Varaiya identified "the precise boundary between decidability and undecidability" of reachability. **Initialized rectangular automata** are decidable with an optimal PSPACE algorithm. In these automata a variable must be reset whenever its dynamics change. Adding **a single stopwatch** to a timed automaton makes reachability undecidable ([Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/1d3a3aff-ca63-4895-a309-708a713aebc2)). Reachability is also undecidable for general linear hybrid automata ([Brihaye et al., arXiv 1104.5335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.5335)). The o-minimal hybrid systems of Lafferriere, Pappas and Sastry always have finite bisimulations, but only because they force a reset at every discrete jump ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225794935_O-Minimal_Hybrid_Systems)). The resets are what matter for circuits. In a switched-mode power supply, the inductor current and capacitor voltage carry across every switching event without being reset. That makes it a non-initialized hybrid system, so the general safety question is **undecidable before nonlinearity is even considered**.

Bounding time helps, but only partly. Time-bounded reachability becomes decidable for non-initialized rectangular automata with non-negative rates, which includes stopwatch automata. It becomes undecidable again once rates can take both signs or diagonal constraints appear, by reduction from two-counter machines ([Brihaye et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.5335)). An inductor current that rises in one mode and falls in the other has exactly that pair of signs.

The second impossibility result concerns the functions that appear in device equations. **Richardson's theorem** shows that for expressions built from π, ln 2, exp and sin, deciding whether an expression is negative is undecidable ([Wikipedia: Richardson's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richardson's_theorem)). Shockley diodes, BJTs and subthreshold MOSFETs contribute exp, and sinusoidal stimuli contribute sin. Exact symbolic verification of a diode or BJT circuit under AC drive therefore falls outside any complete decision procedure.

The third comes from probability. Gaussian noise has unbounded support, so under a Gaussian noise model **no finite worst-case output bound is sound**. There is always a nonzero probability of exceeding it. The workable options are probabilistic: statistical model checking with fixed confidence ([Legay, Delahaye, Bensalem, arXiv 1005.1327](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1327)), as Clarke, Donzé and Legay applied to a Δ-Σ modulator ([FMSD](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10703-009-0076-y)). The other option is to truncate the noise to ±kσ and state the residual probability as an explicit assumption. For LTI circuits, output noise stays Gaussian with a computable variance, so exact analytic propagation is also available.

A related question is not proven impossible but is still open. Whether asymptotic stability of polynomial ODEs is decidable at all remains unknown. Arnold conjectured it is not, and exhibited a family whose stability boundary is not semialgebraic ([Ahmadi and Parrilo, MIT PDF](https://web.mit.edu/~a_a_a/Public/Publications/poly_stability.pdf)). Counting limit cycles, which is the question behind "does this circuit oscillate, and how many ways", is Hilbert's 16th problem. It is unsolved for every degree above one, and even for quadratic fields only a lower bound of four is known ([Scholarpedia](http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Limit_cycles_of_planar_polynomial_vector_fields)).

### Provably expensive: decidable, with a proven cost

Polynomial constraints over the reals are decidable by Tarski's procedure and Collins's cylindrical algebraic decomposition. Davenport and Heintz proved that real quantifier elimination can require **doubly exponential** time: formulas linear in length whose quantifier-free equivalents are doubly exponential in the number of quantifiers ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074771718880004X)). z3's nlsat procedure is a complete decision procedure for quantifier-free polynomial real arithmetic ([Jovanović and de Moura, IJCAR 2012](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-31365-3_27)). It builds on the CAD idea, and in practice it solves problems with a handful of variables quickly. Stability is also provably hard. Deciding local or global asymptotic stability of **cubic** vector fields is **strongly NP-hard**, as are boundedness of trajectories and existence of a quadratic Lyapunov function ([Ahmadi and Parrilo](https://web.mit.edu/~a_a_a/Public/Publications/poly_stability.pdf)). Lyapunov-based methods are also provably incomplete. Ahmadi, Krstić and Parrilo gave a 2-D globally asymptotically stable polynomial system that has **no polynomial Lyapunov function** at all ([PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~parrilo/pubs/files/AhmadiKrsticParrilo-A%20globally%20asymptotically%20stable%20polynomial%20vector%20field%20with%20no%20polynomial%20Lyapunov%20function.pdf)).

### Hard in practice: no theorem, just loss of precision

Most analog ODEs have no closed-form solution, so tools compute over-approximations of the reachable set. Two effects degrade them. The first is the **wrapping effect**: if the set representation is kept small, error accumulates step after step "to the point of becoming useless" ([Maler, arXiv 1403.0952](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.0952)). The second is the **curse of dimensionality**. Fixed-grid hybridization of nonlinear systems is "impractical beyond 3 dimensions", and Maler concludes that nonlinear and hybrid reachability "is still waiting for several conceptual and algorithmic breakthroughs" ([Maler](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.0952)). For **linear** systems the problem is largely solved. Le Guernic and Girard's lazy representation avoids wrapping entirely, and a prototype computed 1,000 steps for a 200-state linear system in about two minutes ([Maler](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.0952)).

The specification problem is equally practical. Analog requirements are quantitative, dense-time and tolerance-laden: settling time, overshoot, −3 dB frequency, ripple, THD. Signal Temporal Logic handles the time-domain ones ([AMT, FORMATS 2007](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-75454-1_22)). Frequency-domain properties need separate treatment, as Clarke, Donzé and Legay found for their Δ-Σ modulator ([FMSD](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10703-009-0076-y)). Finally, every result is a theorem about a **model**: an ideal RC, a Shockley diode, or Circuit OS's 100 Ω MCU. Interval parameters and bounded disturbances can absorb bounded model error. They cannot cover effects that are structurally absent from the model, such as a parasitic inductance that adds a resonance. The project's bench accuracy gate, which requires ngspice to match a physical RC filter within 15%, exists for this reason.

### Why more compute does not close the gap

The three classes respond to compute in completely different ways. For undecidable questions the answer is **zero**. No algorithm exists, so no cluster runs it, and any tool that "solves" such a problem has either restricted the question or can return "unknown". For doubly exponential problems, compute buys very little: each extra quantified variable squares the cost, so a thousand times more hardware adds perhaps one variable. For practical obstacles, abstraction consistently beats hardware. Frehse, Krogh and Rutenbar's forward reachability on a 3rd-order VCO took 1,567 s and 941 MB and still failed to prove the invariant. The finer partition needed for the proof was "prohibitively expensive", with up to 64 times more partitions. Forward/backward refinement proved it in 1,260 s and 500 MB ([DATE 2006 PDF](https://www-verimag.imag.fr/~frehse/frehsekr_date06.pdf)). Althoff et al. could not verify a PLL's thousands of lock cycles with standard hybrid reachability. They "continuized" the switching into a discrete-time model with uncertain parameters, and each case then took under a minute ([ICCAD 2011 PDF](https://arajhans.github.io/files/papers/AlthoffRK+_ICCAD11.pdf)). The lever in every case is a better **abstraction**, **bounds** on parameters and horizon, and a choice of **property** that lands in a decidable fragment. Circuit OS does not need to build computational power. It needs to make these choices well and state them explicitly.

| Obstacle | Class | Applies to | What makes progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reachability of linear or non-initialized hybrid automata | Provably impossible | Comparators, saturation modes, switching converters | Bounded time with restricted rates, over-approximation that may return "unknown", barrier certificates |
| exp and sin together (Richardson) | Provably impossible (exact) | Diode or BJT circuits under sinusoidal drive | δ-decision (dReal), piecewise-linear device models, bounding transcendental constants |
| Sound worst case under Gaussian noise | Provably impossible | Any noise spec | ±kσ truncation as a stated assumption, statistical model checking, analytic LTI noise |
| Polynomial real arithmetic | Doubly exponential worst case | Tolerance proofs on component values | Keep variable count small, exploit monotonicity |
| Stability of polynomial ODEs | Strongly NP-hard (cubic); decidability open | Large-signal nonlinear loops | Linearize and use Routh-Hurwitz; SOS certificates (incomplete) |
| Wrapping, dimensionality | Practically hard | Nonlinear transients, long horizons | Zonotopes and support functions for linear parts, continuization, refinement |
| Model versus hardware | Epistemic | Every result | Interval parameters, reachset conformance, bench validation |

## Nine technique families trade guarantees for reach

The techniques differ most in **what a positive answer guarantees**. Their scale matters less, because published sound results cluster at **2 to 5 continuous states**. Frehse's tunnel-diode oscillator has 2, the VCO 3, and Althoff's PLL 5 ([DATE 2006](https://www-verimag.imag.fr/~frehse/frehsekr_date06.pdf); [ICCAD 2011](https://arajhans.github.io/files/papers/AlthoffRK+_ICCAD11.pdf)). That is comparable to or larger than every Circuit OS template.

### Exact decision procedures over real arithmetic

The circuit property is written as a first-order formula over the reals, for example "there exist R1 and R2 within tolerance such that Vout leaves [lo, hi]". An exact solver then decides it. An UNSAT answer is a proof over the entire tolerance box. z3's nlsat is complete for quantifier-free polynomial arithmetic but does not handle transcendental functions ([IJCAR 2012](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-31365-3_27)). A closely related line encodes device I-V curves as conservative unions of polytopes and solves with SAT. Miller and Brewer used this to prove steady-state bounds across variation without algebraic device models. Their divider test case, two 1 kΩ ±5% resistors from 1.5 V, gives outward-rounded bounds of **0.7125 V to 0.7875 V**. A 14-transistor differential amplifier took 1.7 s to 397 s depending on model accuracy, and a 10-cell SRAM array with 75k constraints took about 20 minutes. The method is limited to steady state ([DATE 2013 PDF](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2013/PDFFILES/10.5_3.PDF)). Walter et al. unrolled hybrid Petri-net models into SMT formulas for bounded model checking. That approach finds violations within k steps but is not an unbounded proof ([ATVA 2007](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-75596-8_7)). **Guarantee:** exact, and sound as long as the encoding is exact. **Scale:** small numbers of variables, and steady-state problems with tens of transistors. **Python:** `z3-solver` everywhere, with sympy to derive the equations.

### δ-complete decision procedures

Gao, Avigad and Clarke avoid undecidability by answering a weaker question. Given a formula and δ > 0, the procedure returns either "unsat" or "the δ-weakened formula is sat". This is decidable, in NP relative to the function oracles, for bounded formulas over polynomials, transcendental functions and Lipschitz ODEs ([arXiv 1204.3513](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3513)). **"unsat" is sound with no δ caveat**, and for verification unsat means safe. "δ-sat" is not a counterexample, because it may be a false alarm near the boundary. dReal implements this ([CADE 2013](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-38574-2_14)), and exists-forall formulas, the shape of "for all tolerances, the spec holds", also have a δ-decision procedure ([Kong, Solar-Lezama, Gao, CAV 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.08137)). iSAT combines CDCL with interval propagation for the same undecidable theory and can return "unknown" ([iSAT retrospective](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-16855-9_18)). **Python:** `pip install dreal` needs the IBEX and CLP system libraries and lists only Ubuntu and macOS, at version 4.21.06.2 ([GitHub dreal4](https://github.com/dreal/dreal4)). For Circuit OS it would run inside the Linux container, not on the Windows development machine.

### Interval and affine arithmetic

Every quantity is carried as a range, and each operation is rounded outward. The result is a guaranteed enclosure. Plain intervals suffer from the **dependency problem**: a divider's R2 appears in both the numerator and the denominator, and the two occurrences are treated as independent, so the bound comes out loose. Affine arithmetic tracks first-order correlations between noise symbols and reduces that looseness ([TEMA tutorial](https://tema.sbmac.org.br/tema/article/view/352)). Radojicic et al. propagated affine ranges through simulation and checked assertions on them ([VLSI Design 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2013/239064)). The Hannover group used affine arithmetic for sizing and to speed up reachability ([bibliography in the same paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2013/239064)). **Guarantee:** sound, possibly loose. **Scale:** cheap, linear in circuit size. For monotone expressions such as the divider, evaluating the tolerance corners is exact, which removes the looseness entirely.

### Set-based reachability

The tool pushes a whole set of states (a zonotope, polytope, support function or Taylor model) through the ODE or hybrid automaton, which gives a sound over-approximation of every trajectory from every initial state and parameter value. Zonotope reachability for linear systems handles "several hundred" state variables ([arXiv 1712.00369](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.00369)). Althoff et al. verified a 27 GHz charge-pump PLL with a 5-element state and ±1% charge-pump currents. The guaranteed lock time was 1,652 to 2,039 cycles, against a worst case of 994 to 1,271 in 30 random simulations, and the computation took 42.9 to 55.0 s per initial-phase interval ([ICCAD 2011](https://arajhans.github.io/files/papers/AlthoffRK+_ICCAD11.pdf)). That gap between the guaranteed bound and the sampled worst case shows how conservative the result is. Frehse's PHAVer uses exact rational arithmetic on linear hybrid automata ([DATE 2006](https://www-verimag.imag.fr/~frehse/frehsekr_date06.pdf)). Transistor circuits are handled by abstracting devices into piecewise-linear hybrid automata ([Tarraf and Hedrich, DATE 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332268721_Behavioral_Modeling_of_Transistor-Level_Circuits_using_Automatic_Abstraction_to_Hybrid_Automata)). Nonlinear Taylor-model tools proved fragile on stiff circuit ODEs. Flow* failed on all four CMOS gate models in a 2018 comparison, with division by zero, segmentation faults, or runs killed after 60 minutes ([Fan et al., arXiv 1803.02975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02975)). **Maintained tools:** CORA (MATLAB, with a 2026 manual) and ReachabilityAnalysis.jl (Julia, MIT licence, supporting parametric uncertainty and polynomial zonotopes) ([CORA](https://tumcps.github.io/CORA/); [GitHub](https://github.com/JuliaReach/ReachabilityAnalysis.jl)). Julia can be called from Python. CORA needs a MATLAB licence, which fits badly with a Dockerized AGPL service.

### Discretized model checking, hybrid Petri nets and equivalence checking

Hartong, Hedrich and Barke divided a nonlinear circuit's state space into boxes, built a transition system from sampled vector fields, and model-checked CTL-style properties on it ([CAV 2002](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-45657-0_33)). Steinhorst and Hedrich's Analog Specification Language made oscillation and start-up time checkable ([DATE 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4325671_Model_Checking_of_Analog_Systems_using_an_Analog_Specification_Language)). The abstraction is sampled rather than fully conservative, and box counts grow exponentially with dimension. Myers's group built labeled hybrid Petri nets and the LEMA tool, which generates models from SPICE traces and verifies them by zone exploration. LEMA was applied to DACs, phase interpolators, VCOs and switched-capacitor integrators, and activity ends around 2017 ([Myers group](https://async.ece.utah.edu/research/formal-verification/)). Analog equivalence checking compares two descriptions over a state-space region, but Althoff et al. note that current techniques are "typically semi-formal", so a complete enclosure is not guaranteed ([ICCAD 2011](https://arajhans.github.io/files/papers/AlthoffRK+_ICCAD11.pdf)). For Circuit OS these are mostly of methodological interest. The idea worth borrowing is equivalence checking of the compiler itself: confirm that the SPICE netlist compiled from the IR behaves like the IR's own symbolic model over the tolerance ranges.

### Simulation with sound error bounds

This family runs a finite number of numerical simulations and "bloats" each one by a **discrepancy function**, a bound on how far neighbouring trajectories can diverge. The union is a sound reachtube. The method is "sound, complete for robust invariant verification". It can fail to decide if the discrepancy is too conservative ([Fan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02975)). Fan et al. verified transistor-level CMOS gates whose ODEs have hundreds of exp and log terms in 9 to 2,294 s. They also state that larger circuits remain future work ([arXiv 1803.02975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02975)). C2E2's last release was in 2020 ([GitHub](https://github.com/C2E2-Development-Team/C2E2-Tool)). DryVR learns the discrepancy from data, which makes the guarantee probabilistic rather than sound ([arXiv 1702.06902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.06902)). **Reachset conformance** is a related idea that fits Circuit OS. Non-determinism is added to a piecewise-linear circuit model until it provably encloses all recorded simulation traces, and that model is then verified formally ([Kochdumper et al., ASP-DAC 2020](https://em.cs.uni-frankfurt.de/index.php?L=1&id=46); [CORA conformance, ARCH 2023](https://easychair.org/publications/paper/Zmbv)). Soundness requires the actual ODE. For Circuit OS that means the MNA equations derived from the IR, not ngspice treated as a black box.

### Certificates: Lyapunov, barrier, SOS, LMI and robust-stability tests

Instead of computing reachable sets, these methods search for a function that proves the property. A **barrier certificate** B(x) is non-positive on the initial set, positive on the unsafe set, and non-increasing along the dynamics. For polynomial systems, sum-of-squares relaxation turns the search into a convex semidefinite program ([Prajna and Jadbabaie, HSCC 2004](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24743-2_32)). A property like "inductor current never exceeds I_max" fits this form. The soundness trap is numerical. An SDP solver reporting success "does not have to be a valid sum-of-squares certificate" ([Löfberg, IEEE TAC 2009](https://yalmip.github.io/reference/lofberg2009/)). Floating-point certificates need exact or rational post-checking to count as proofs ([Roux, Voronin, Sankaranarayanan, SAS 2016](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-53413-7_21)). For linear circuits, classical tests are exact. **Kharitonov's theorem** decides stability of an interval polynomial family with **four** vertex checks, regardless of degree, when coefficients vary independently ([Bhattacharyya et al., ch. 5](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/spb/books/robustcontrol/chapter05.pdf)). Routh-Hurwitz decides stability of a single polynomial from sign conditions, in exact rational arithmetic. Circuit coefficients are products of component values, so they are correlated, and Kharitonov over a coefficient box is sound but conservative. **Python:** CVXPY (with SCS or CVXOPT) for LMIs and hand-built SOS, and the picos-based `SumOfSquares` package ([review of SOS tooling](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/168023/1/root.pdf); [CVXPY](https://www.cvxpy.org/)). SOSTOOLS and YALMIP are MATLAB.

### Theorem proving

These methods carry the strongest guarantee and are the least automatic. Narayanan et al. modelled noise as stochastic differential equations, derived closed forms, and proved properties under 0.18 µm process variation with **MetiTarski**. The op-amp integrator took **3.52 s** and the bandgap reference **8.58 s** after rescaling. The authors state the method is "limited to linear time-invariant designs" and "involves several manual steps" ([Cambridge PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~lp15/papers/Arith/Narayanan%20DATE.pdf)). HOL Light formalizations of the Laplace transform verify transfer functions of linear circuits ([LPAR 2013](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-45221-5_50)), and FASiM does this automatically for Simulink models including Sallen-Key filters ([arXiv 2001.06702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.06702)). Transfer-function correctness of LTI filters is the most mature formally verified analog class. **KeYmaera X** proves properties of parametric hybrid programs in differential dynamic logic from a small trusted axiomatic core ([CADE 2015](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-21401-6_36)). It is the natural target for switching converters, but it is interactive or tactic-driven. None of these tools has Python bindings, so each would be a subprocess integration.

### Semi-formal: STL monitoring, falsification and statistical checking

STL expresses analog time-domain requirements over continuous traces. Its robustness semantics gives a margin: positive means satisfied by that much, and negative means violated ([rtamt](https://github.com/nickovic/rtamt)). Monitoring checks simulated traces only. Falsification tools such as S-TaLiRo minimize robustness over inputs to hunt for violations, but **failing to find one is not a proof** ([TACAS 2011](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-19835-9_21)). Industry sign-off works at this level: SystemVerilog real-number models with assertions and coverage, for example a SAR ADC model simulated in 0.5 s against 20 s for Verilog-AMS ([JETTA 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10836-024-06124-8)). **Python:** `rtamt` on PyPI, the `signal-temporal-logic` package, and PSY-TaLiRo for falsification ([arXiv 2106.02200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02200)). These plug directly into ngspice transient output in the existing Celery pipeline.

### What each answer is worth

The families form a ladder of trust, and a Circuit OS claim should always name the rung it stands on. This ordering is a synthesis of the sources above, not a ranking from a single paper.

| Rung | Technique | What "pass" means | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Theorem prover (HOL Light, KeYmaera X, MetiTarski) | Checked proof from axioms | Manual modelling steps sit outside the proof |
| 2 | Exact decision (z3 nlsat, exact Routh-Hurwitz, Kharitonov) | Exact truth over the whole parameter box | Polynomial fragment only; cost explodes with variables |
| 3 | Outward-rounded interval or affine arithmetic, dReal "unsat", sound reachability | Sound enclosure | May return "unknown" or a loose bound |
| 4 | SOS or LMI certificates | Sound only after rational re-verification | Floating-point SDP output alone is not a proof |
| 5 | SMT bounded model checking | No violation within k steps | Says nothing beyond k |
| 6 | STL monitoring of simulated traces | Those traces satisfy the property, with a margin | Other parameter values are not covered |
| 7 | Falsification, Monte Carlo, DryVR | Evidence only | Absence of a counterexample proves nothing |

## Circuit OS sits almost entirely in decidable territory

The Phase 1 templates avoid nearly every impossibility result above. The divider and the DC parts of the sensor boards are **algebraic**, with no dynamics at all. The RC filter is a **first-order linear ODE** with a rational transfer function. The LED is a static nonlinearity, and a datasheet V_f range makes it linear in the unknowns. None of them has discrete modes, so the hybrid-automaton undecidability does not apply. The practical recommendation is a deterministic **proof compiler** alongside the existing SPICE, KiCad and BOM compilers. It would take the IR, derive symbolic MNA equations with sympy, attach tolerance intervals from `component_constraints.py`, and emit a z3 query of the form "there exist component values within tolerance that violate the spec". UNSAT is a proof. SAT returns a concrete counterexample, which ngspice can then replay as a cross-check. This keeps The One Rule intact, because the LLM never writes the proof obligation, and it upgrades the current 15% grader from a single nominal ngspice run to a statement about every component value in tolerance.

Three details decide whether Tier 0 and Tier 1 claims hold up. First, **exploit monotonicity**. Vout = Vin·R2/(R1+R2) is monotone in each resistor, so its four tolerance corners give the exact worst case, and the z3 query becomes a certified cross-check rather than the only argument. Second, **handle π explicitly**. A formula containing f_c = 1/(2πRC) leaves the polynomial fragment unless π is bracketed by rational bounds. With that bracket z3 stays exact, and the alternative is dReal's δ-semantics. Third, **state the model**. A proof that the LED current stays below 40 mA holds for the V_f range, the V_OH range and the resistor tolerance written into the IR. It says nothing about V_f drift outside that range, and the Arduino proofs hold for a 100 Ω MCU, not the ATmega328P. Reachset conformance is the principled way to tighten this link later. It fits a model to ngspice or bench traces with enough non-determinism to enclose them, and then proves properties of that model ([CORA](https://tumcps.github.io/CORA/)).

Two tooling constraints shape the stack. The development machine runs Windows, and dReal does not list Windows support ([GitHub dreal4](https://github.com/dreal/dreal4)), so δ-decision work belongs in the Linux Celery workers. z3, sympy, CVXPY and rtamt install everywhere with pip. The project is licensed AGPL-3.0, which makes MIT-licensed JuliaReach a better reachability engine to depend on than MATLAB-bound CORA. For Tiers 0 through 2, a few hundred lines of NumPy interval or zonotope code over LTI MNA systems would probably be simpler than any external reachability tool. That is an inference, since no source benchmarked it.

## The loop is prior art; the analog front end is the open niche

Every stage of the proposed "LLM → formulate goal → choose abstraction or solver → run engine → interpret → refine" loop has published precedent. **Goal formulation:** nl2spec translates natural language to temporal logic and maps subformulas back to text so humans can catch mistranslations ([CAV 2023](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-37703-7_18)). NL-to-STL work has reached 16,000-sample datasets ([Findings of ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.544.pdf)), and ReasonSTL targets physical units and arithmetic ([arXiv 2605.06483](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06483)), which are exactly the weaknesses analog specs expose. **Solver choice:** SOVER routes exact checks to z3 and tolerance-aware nonlinear checks to dReal ([arXiv 2609.00728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00728)). **Propose-check-refine:** Neural Lyapunov Control ([NeurIPS 2019](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8587-neural-lyapunov-control)), Fossil 2.0 ([HSCC 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3641513.3651398)), and BarrierBench, an LLM agent reporting over 90% success on 100 systems with SMT checking ([L4DC 2026, arXiv 2511.09363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09363)). **Soundness of orchestration:** Lemur proves an LLM-plus-verifier calculus sound ([ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0c86142265c5e2c900613dd1d031cb90-Abstract-Conference.html)). The closest neighbour is Kabra, Laurent, Martins, Mitsch and Platzer. Their LLMs drive **KeYmaera X** proofs for hybrid systems and succeeded on 5 of 5 hard verification case studies ([arXiv 2603.00737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00737)). A claim of novelty for the loop itself, or for "proof orchestration for continuous or hybrid systems", will not survive review.

The gap is on the analog side. Every LLM analog-design agent examined validates by **simulation, rule-based ERC or LLM-as-judge**, never by a formal engine. That list covers AnalogCoder ([AAAI 2025](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/32016)), CircuitLM, whose authors concede that ERC "cannot guarantee the circuit works" ([arXiv 2601.04505](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04505v2)), and MenTeR, AnalogAgent, AutoSizer, EEsizer, AnaFlow and SABLE. LLM-generated formal properties are a crowded field for **digital** RTL: FVEval is explicitly a digital benchmark ([arXiv 2410.23299](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23299)), and an ASP-DAC 2026 survey of LLM-assisted verification covers digital formal work ([PDF](https://www.aspdac.com/aspdac2026/archive/pdf/3D-4.pdf)). No work was found that generates analog or AMS formal properties with an LLM. The searches did not examine commercial EDA products, and absence from a search is evidence, not proof.

The defensible claim is therefore an **integration claim scoped to analog circuits**. A version that survives scrutiny reads: "LLM-guided formal verification of bounded properties of board-level analog circuits, in which a deterministic compiler derives the verification model from a typed IR, and every guarantee is machine-checked by an exact or δ-complete solver over explicitly stated component models, parameter bounds and time horizons." The second phrasing the user proposed, "machine-checkable guarantees over explicitly defined models and bounds", is the right shape. Its strength depends on actually stating the model, the bounds and the solver semantics for every result.

### Pitfalls to design out from the start

The **specification gap** is the most serious risk. A proof of the wrong formula is worse than no proof, because it produces false confidence. The SVA literature finds that LLM approaches focus on syntactic correctness and rarely evaluate semantic strength ([SpecSyn, arXiv 2604.21570](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21570v1)). The defence is nl2spec-style back-translation: render the formal property as plain English, such as "for every R in 1,574–1,606 Ω and C in 90–110 nF, f_c lies within 850–1,150 Hz", and require user sign-off before the proof counts. Those ranges are the 1% resistor and 10% capacitor already named in the project's accuracy gate. Worked by hand, the corners give roughly 900 to 1,125 Hz, so the property holds. With a 5% resistor instead, the upper corner reaches about 1,170 Hz and the proof would correctly fail. A single nominal ngspice run would not reveal this.

**Vacuous and weakened properties** are the second risk. Assertions that pass trivially are a documented failure in LLM formal flows, and Krishnamurthy et al. argue that "quality-aware closure, rather than assertion generation alone, is the missing step". Their fixes are mutation-guided weakness detection and solver selection ([arXiv 2606.21451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21451)). The "refine and retry" step in the proposed loop carries a specific hazard. An LLM that sees "property failed" can make it pass by weakening the property, for example widening the band or shortening the horizon. The goal should therefore be **frozen after sign-off**. Refinement may change the abstraction, the solver or the certificate template, never the property. Each proven property should also be mutation-tested by injecting a deliberately wrong component value, such as C = 1 nF in the RC filter, which is already a launch-checklist item. The property must then fail.

**Solver semantics** must be reported honestly. A dReal "δ-sat" is not a counterexample, a floating-point SOS solution is not a certificate until re-verified in exact arithmetic ([Löfberg](https://yalmip.github.io/reference/lofberg2009/)), and a monitored STL trace is not a proof. **The LLM must stay outside the trusted computing base.** Following Lemur, Fossil and the KeYmaera X work, the LLM proposes and the checker disposes. For Circuit OS this means the LLM emits a property or certificate candidate as JSON against a typed schema, and a deterministic compiler turns it into the solver query. The LLM also adds little to solver choice at Tier 0 to 2. A deterministic dispatcher keyed on circuit class (algebraic, LTI, piecewise-affine, switched) is more reliable and easier to defend, and LLM selection is worth its cost only at Tier 3 and above. Finally, reviewers will ask for a **no-LLM baseline**: the same engines with hand-written properties. The LLM's contribution should be measured as coverage, time to a correct property, or success at finding certificates, not as whether proofs exist at all.

## Conclusion

Phase 1 does not need a breakthrough, only a well-chosen fragment. Exact proofs over full component tolerance for the divider, LED, sensor-board DC checks and the RC filter are a days-to-weeks engineering task with z3 and sympy, and linear op-amp stages extend the same machinery. The hard results matter only once modes and switching arrive, and at that point the right move is to restrict the question to bounded horizons, over-approximations and certificates, not to buy hardware. The difficulty therefore falls in the reverse order of what one might expect. The formal engines are the easy part for these circuits. The hard, and defensible, part is the front end: compiling an IR into a faithful model with explicit bounds, and making sure the property the LLM formalizes is the property the user meant.

The strategic implication is that Circuit OS's existing architecture is already its best argument. "LLM writes JSON, deterministic compilers do the rest" is the same propose/dispose split that makes Lemur and Fossil sound, extended to a proof compiler. A credible v1 claim is narrow and checkable: every Phase 1 design ships with a machine-checked statement of which properties hold for which component ranges under which model, alongside the ngspice result and the bench gate that tests the model itself. That is a first for LLM analog design as far as these searches reach. Broader claims about hybrid or power circuits should wait until the switched-system tier produces results worth showing.
